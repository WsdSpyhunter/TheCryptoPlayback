Top story

Bitcoin's slide below $58K collided with a wave of regulatory, legal, and infrastructure news that shows the industry building even as the market bleeds.

Here’s A Story You May Have Missed!

Florida Man Used AI to Deepfake a Streamer Into a $260K Meme Coin Scam

David Anthony Sanchez used AI to pose as streamer N3ON in order to persuade a meme coin project's organizers to pay him for a fake endorsement deal, netting $260K before he was jailed. It's a strange little case study in how AI impersonation tools have made celebrity-endorsement fraud cheaper and easier to pull off in crypto's meme coin corner. The scam needed no real endorsement, no real streamer, and apparently almost worked anyway.
Read more at Decrypt →

Strategic Bitcoin Reserve Bill Clears Its First Big Hurdle

Rep. Nick Begich's bill to codify the Strategic Bitcoin Reserve into law — complete with a 20-year ban on selling the government's bitcoin — cleared a House committee vote and is now eligible for a full floor vote.

A 20-year sale ban is the kind of detail that turns a symbolic gesture into an actual commitment, assuming it survives the floor. Committee approval is progress, but it's still a long way from a president's desk.

Read more at Bitcoin Magazine →

DOJ Calls Its Crypto Fraud Sweep the 'Largest Ever'

Following President Trump's directive to crack down on fraud, the Justice Department announced what it called its 'largest-ever crackdown' on billions in crypto fraud proceeds.

Crypto fraud enforcement has historically lagged the scale of the problem, so a headline-grabbing crackdown is notable regardless of how the politics around it shake out. Whether it meaningfully dents the scam economy is a separate question from whether it makes good copy.

Read more at The Block →

Bitcoin Sinks Below $58,000 as Inflation, Tariffs and AI Selloff Pile On

Bitcoin fell below $58,000 as stronger U.S. growth, accelerating inflation and proposed tariffs coincided with a renewed AI stock selloff and bets on higher interest rates in December and beyond.

It's a lot of macro weight landing on crypto at once — tariffs, inflation, rate expectations, and an AI stock rout all arriving in the same week. Untangling which force matters most is mostly an academic exercise when they're all pulling in the same direction.

Read more at Decrypt →

Bitcoin ETFs Set a New Record — for Outflows

US spot Bitcoin ETFs have recorded $4.94 billion in November net outflows, exceeding the previous monthly record of $4.51 billion set in June.

ETFs were supposed to be the stabilizing force that brought in patient institutional money. A new outflow record suggests that thesis is being tested in real time, not confirmed.

Read more at Cointelegraph →

Ukraine Finally Legalizes Crypto — Taxes Included

Ukraine passed a law to legalize and tax its crypto market starting in July 2027. The law taxes crypto profits at 18% plus a 5% military levy, cuts the rate for older holdings, and puts stablecoins under the central bank.

A war-era government choosing to formally tax and regulate crypto rather than simply ignore it is a reminder that legal clarity and state revenue needs tend to travel together, even under the most difficult circumstances.

Read more at Decrypt →

France Backs Off Forcing Bitcoiners to Declare Their Wallets

Holders had warned that reporting self-custody wallets worth over €100,000 could expose them to risk in a country hit by a wave of crypto kidnappings. France's Senate rejected the plan.

Given France's own recent spate of physical crypto kidnappings, a mandatory wallet-disclosure registry was always going to be a tough sell on safety grounds alone, whatever the tax rationale behind it.

Read more at Decrypt →

Ethereum Moves Up Its Quantum Deadline Over AI Fears

Ethlabs co-founder Julian Ma told Cointelegraph that AI poses a more immediate threat than quantum computing, as Ethereum prepares one of its biggest security overhauls since the Merge, backing Keccak-256 to counter both.

It's a telling admission that the near-term threat model for blockchain security is shifting from theoretical quantum computers years out to AI tools available right now.

Read more at Cointelegraph →

Someone Is Hijacking Miners' Hashrate Through Unencrypted Connections

Braiins warns that an attacker is redirecting bitcoin miners' compute power to steal mining rewards by exploiting unencrypted connections.

It's a basic security hygiene problem rather than some exotic exploit, which somehow makes it more unsettling — a lot of real mining revenue appears to be riding on connections that were never locked down.

Read more at The Block →

HRF Funds Seven Bitcoin Projects for People Living Under Authoritarian Rule

HRF's Bitcoin Development Fund backed seven projects for self-custody, privacy and education under repression, ranging from phone wallets to solar mining in Nigeria.

This is the use case that tends to get lost in ETF-flow headlines and price charts: tools built for people who need censorship resistance as a matter of daily survival, not portfolio allocation.
