The Clarity Act Is Dead. Long Live the SEC and CFTC.

The Clarity Act failed to advance in the Senate, and with it went Congress's best shot at a comprehensive market-structure framework for crypto. In its absence, the SEC and CFTC are stepping into the vacuum, with the CFTC already submitting its own crypto rulemaking to the White House for review.

There's something almost fitting about this: an industry that spent years complaining about 'regulation by enforcement' now gets 'regulation by agency initiative' instead of an act of Congress. It might actually move faster this way, but faster isn't the same as more durable — agency rules can be rewritten by the next administration in a way a statute can't.

Bitcoin's Rip Back Toward $80K Was Basically a Short Squeeze

Bitcoin blasted 5.88% higher in a single session, tearing back toward its 2026 highs, in what Decrypt describes as a fresh short squeeze rather than a broad wave of new buying. Reclaiming $80,000 came even as the Clarity Act stalled and rate hikes weighed on sentiment elsewhere.

A move powered mostly by liquidating shorts tells you more about positioning than conviction. It's a real price move, sure, but it's worth remembering that squeezes can unwind just as sharply as they build.

Wall Street's Hottest Crypto Trade Right Now Is... Privacy Coins

Grayscale's Zcash ETF has pulled in more than $233 million in under a month, and the firm is now splitting shares three ways to make the red-hot fund more accessible to a wider set of buyers.

It's a genuinely odd twist: after years of regulators treating privacy tech with suspicion, one of the more aggressively private assets in the space is becoming a mainstream ETF product. Worth watching whether this appetite is about Zcash specifically or a broader institutional itch for privacy-preserving rails.

Treasury Sanctions the Exchange Behind Iran's Bitcoin 'Tolls'

The US Treasury sanctioned BitBank, an Iranian crypto exchange OFAC says moved hundreds of millions of dollars in Bitcoin to the Revolutionary Guards over just two months, tied to tolls collected from ships in the Strait of Hormuz.

This is the uncomfortable flip side of Bitcoin's borderless design showing up in a geopolitically loaded context. It's also a reminder that 'crypto is used for crime' headlines usually understate how much of that crime is state-level, not retail-level.

Coinbase and Kalshi Both Want to Sell You Leveraged Apple and Tesla Bets

Coinbase filed to bring 24/5 perpetual futures on individual US stocks like Apple, Tesla and Nvidia to American traders, seeking CFTC approval for leveraged exposure without actual share ownership. Kalshi filed a similar proposal for perpetual futures tied to individual stocks days later.

Crypto-native exchanges racing to offer perpetual futures on Big Tech names is a pretty clean signal of where this industry sees its growth: less 'replace the banks' and more 'out-derivative the derivatives desk.' Whether regulators are comfortable with 24/5 leveraged stock exposure outside traditional market hours is a very open question.

Report: Lagarde Personally Intervened to Block Binance's EU License

According to a Wall Street Journal report cited by CoinDesk, ECB President Christine Lagarde intervened to block Binance from securing a MiCA license in the EU.

If accurate, this is a notable escalation — not a technical compliance rejection from a national regulator, but apparently direct pressure from the top of Europe's central bank. It's a good reminder that MiCA's promise of a single, predictable EU rulebook still runs through very human, very political decision-making at the highest levels.

S&P Global Buys OpenZeppelin, Betting Big on On-Chain Security

S&P Global agreed to acquire smart-contract security firm OpenZeppelin, which will keep operating as a standalone unit within the ratings giant, as part of a broader push into on-chain infrastructure.

A legacy ratings agency buying a smart-contract auditor is a fairly loud statement about where 'traditional finance goes on-chain' actually starts: not with flashy tokenized products, but with the unglamorous plumbing of security and risk assessment.
