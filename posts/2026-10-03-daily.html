Uptober Delivers, Blast Doesn't

TOP STORY

Bitcoin is partying like it's January again while a chunk of the L2 landscape quietly packs up and regulators keep rewriting the rulebook underneath everyone's feet.

Bitcoin Pokes $87K as the Jobs Report Does the Fed's Work For It

A weak September jobs report (just 29,000 jobs added, unemployment ticking up to 4.2%) sent Treasury yields lower and bitcoin briefly tagging $87K, clearing a sell wall that had capped price near $85K. Risk-on sentiment is spilling into the broader market too, with bitcoin dominance creeping back toward 60%.

It's a tidy illustration of how bitcoin trades these days: less 'digital gold narrative,' more 'leveraged bet on what Jerome Powell does next.' Funny how a soft labor market is bullish for the asset that was supposedly going to replace the labor market's currency.

Spot Bitcoin ETFs Just Had Their Second-Best Month Since Last October

US spot bitcoin ETFs pulled in $2.65 billion of net inflows in September, the second-largest monthly haul since October 2025. Institutions, it seems, didn't get the memo that everyone else was supposedly exhausted by the sideways summer.

Steady ETF demand has quietly become the boring-but-important story of this cycle — less dramatic than a $5 trillion Nvidia headline, but arguably more load-bearing for where bitcoin's price floor actually sits.

Read more at The Block →

Blast, Once a $2.3B Ethereum L2, Is Shutting Down

Blast — backed by Paradigm and once one of Ethereum's largest layer-2s by total value locked — is winding down after its assets plunged 98% and operating costs outpaced revenue. Users have until October 26 to withdraw to mainnet.

This is the unglamorous flip side of the L2 gold rush: dozens of chains were launched on the premise that TVL and yield incentives would eventually become a real economy. Blast's obituary is a reminder that 'number go up' token incentives are not the same thing as a sustainable business model.

Community Banks Sue Federal Regulator Over Crypto Trust Charters

A community banking group has filed suit against the OCC, arguing the regulator overstepped its mandate by granting trust bank charters to crypto firms. It's a direct challenge to the idea that crypto companies can access bank-like status without playing by the full set of bank-like rules.

This is the kind of turf war that rarely makes headlines but quietly decides who gets to hold your money and under what rulebook. Expect more of these lawsuits as every incumbent industry tries to slow-walk crypto's march into traditional finance's backyard.

Read more at Cointelegraph →

SEC Floats Letting Funds and Advisers Self-Custody Crypto

The SEC has proposed a framework that would let investment advisers and funds self-custody crypto in certain cases, with state trust companies also eligible to serve as custodians. It's a meaningful crack in the wall that previously pushed most institutional crypto holdings toward a narrow set of approved custodians.

Self-custody for institutions sounds nice in theory, but it also means more entities holding keys with less uniform oversight — which is exactly the kind of fragmentation some industry voices are currently warning about elsewhere in this very newsletter.

Read more at The Block →

BitGo CEO: Clarity's Collapse Left Capital Markets More Exposed Than Lehman

BitGo CEO Mike Belshe warned that the failure of crypto exchange Clarity left systemic risk in its wake, pointing to the danger of one firm combining exchange, brokerage, and custody functions under one roof — a structure he says could be worse than what preceded the Lehman collapse.

Strong words from a custody executive whose entire business model is 'please don't let one company hold everything.' That doesn't make him wrong, though — concentrated, unbundled risk inside a single crypto entity is exactly the kind of thing regulators keep promising to fix and keep not quite fixing.

Read more at The Block →

Circle Tells Brussels: MiCA's Bank-Deposit Rule Is Pushing Big Stablecoins Out

Circle has pushed back on MiCA's mandate that stablecoin issuers hold a chunk of reserves as bank deposits, arguing — alongside the ECB — that the rule and its concentration caps are keeping the largest global stablecoins outside Europe's regulatory perimeter entirely.

There's something almost poetic about a stablecoin issuer lobbying regulators to make rules looser so it can comply with them more easily. Whether that's principled policy advocacy or just commercial self-interest dressed up as market structure concern is, as always, a matter of where you're sitting.

Read more at Decrypt →

$4.2B Crypto Bank Anchorage Digital Cuts 17% of Staff

Anchorage Digital is reportedly cutting 17% of its workforce even as the company expands its institutional footprint. The timing — layoffs alongside growth announcements — is the kind of corporate whiplash that's become routine across the industry.

Crypto institutions are learning the same lesson traditional finance learned decades ago: scaling headcount and scaling revenue are not the same curve, and sometimes the fix for one is cutting the other.

Read more at Cointelegraph →

Ethereum's New zkAPI Lets You Pay for AI Without Anyone Knowing Who You Are

The Ethereum Foundation launched zkAPI, which lets users prepay in USDC and query AI models through zero-knowledge proofs — structured so no single party can see both who's asking and what they're asking. Users deposit into a vault and authorize spending cryptographically.

This is crypto doing the thing it's actually good at: building plumbing for privacy-preserving payments rather than just speculative tokens. Whether anyone outside the crypto-native crowd cares about anonymous AI queries is a separate question, but the engineering is genuinely interesting.
