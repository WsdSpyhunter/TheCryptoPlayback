Crypto Mom Exits, Bitget Bleeds, Washington Still Can't Decide

TOP STORY

Regulators are shuffling chairs, a major exchange just got taken for hundreds of millions, and Strategy wants its dividends paid on weekends too — just another Thursday in crypto.

Hester Peirce, Crypto's Favorite Regulator, Is Leaving the SEC

Hester Peirce — the Commissioner nicknamed 'Crypto Mom' for her years of dissenting against enforcement-first regulation and later leading the SEC's Crypto Task Force — announced she's departing the agency on October 2, after roughly eight years in the role.

This is the industry losing its most reliable institutional ally right as the regulatory framework is still half-built. Whoever replaces her will shape how the next few years of rulemaking actually go, and 'crypto-friendly regulator with real influence' is not a role that gets filled easily.

Bitget Confirms $387.5M Breach, Blames It on Hot Wallets

Bitget has confirmed a $387.5 million security breach hitting its hot wallets, though the exchange says private keys weren't compromised and that losses will be covered by its User Protection Fund.

Nine figures is nine figures, no matter how reassuring the follow-up statement sounds. 'We'll cover it out of the protection fund' is the corporate version of 'don't worry about it,' and users are right to keep asking exactly how the intrusion happened in the first place.

Tether Says Its Exposure to the Seized $89M Bank Was 'Limited'

Following a US asset seizure connected to an $89 million case involving EQIBank, Tether says its own exposure to the bank was minimal, distancing itself from the fallout.

Tether has gotten very good at issuing calm, brief statements every time its name shows up adjacent to a federal action. That practice run is useful — but it's also a reminder that stablecoin issuers now live one banking relationship away from a headline they didn't choose.

Blockchain Association Shakes Up Leadership After CLARITY Act Collapses

Blockchain Association CEO Summer Mersinger is stepping down next month, with Kristin Smith returning as interim CEO — a leadership shift that landed shortly after the crypto CLARITY Act failed to pass.

When a lobbying group's leadership changes right after its signature legislative push flops, that's not a coincidence, that's a scoreboard. The industry's DC strategy clearly needs a rethink, not just a new name at the top.

Read more at The Block →

Fed Floats Reserve Limits and Capital Rules for Stablecoin Issuers

The Federal Reserve has proposed new rules for stablecoin issuers under the GENIUS Act, including reserve-asset limits and standardized capital requirements.

This is the part of stablecoin legalization nobody wanted to talk about at the ribbon-cutting: actual prudential rules with teeth. Issuers who've been enjoying light-touch treatment are about to find out what 'regulated like a bank, minus the deposit insurance' really means in practice.

Read more at The Block →

Strategy Wants Its Preferred Stock to Pay Dividends Every Single Day

Strategy is asking shareholders to approve daily dividend payments across its four preferred stocks — STRC, STRD, STRF, and STRK — moving toward accrual on weekends and holidays, in a bid the company says will smooth out volatility.

It's a clever bit of financial engineering dressed up as investor-friendliness: more frequent payouts, more predictable cash flow optics, and one more lever to keep STRC trading near its target price. Whether daily accrual actually changes investor behavior or just changes the spreadsheet is the open question.

Read more at Cointelegraph →

Kalshi Loses Again, Court Says States Get a Say Over Sports Contracts

Another federal appeals court has ruled against prediction market provider Kalshi, finding that its sports-related contracts are subject to state regulation after all.

Prediction markets have spent the last year selling themselves as a federally-regulated alternative to state-by-state betting law — this ruling says that pitch has limits. Two losses in the same legal argument is starting to look less like bad luck and more like a pattern the courts aren't buying.

Read more at CoinDesk →

Bitcoin Holders Are Cashing Out — Just Quieter Than Usual

Long-term bitcoin holders are selling, but the pattern looks different from previous market tops, according to on-chain analysis of current distribution behavior.

Every cycle has its own flavor of 'is this the top,' and this one apparently comes with less drama than the last. Whether that's a sign of a maturing market or just a slower-motion version of the same old story is exactly the kind of thing that's fun to debate and impossible to prove in real time.
