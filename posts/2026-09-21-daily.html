Bitcoin hits $85K on short squeeze as bears get liquidated en masse

Sometimes the market rewards pessimism by punishing it. Bitcoin's push to $85,000 represents the kind of coordinated liquidation cascade that tends to make the crypto crowd nervous and vindicated in equal measure. According to CoinGlass data, at least $750 million in crypto short positions got liquidated over 24 hours, the vast majority of them bearish bets that simply couldn't weather the momentum.

What's notable here is the mechanics: this isn't some slow grind higher on positive fundamental news or fresh institutional inflows (though those may be lurking). This is a short squeeze—the algorithmic equivalent of stepping on the gas when traders who bet against Bitcoin get forced to cover their positions. When liquidations cascade, they trigger stop-losses and margin calls, which trigger more liquidation, which triggers more selling from the trapped shorts. It's violent and brief, but it clears out a lot of weak hands betting the other way.

The $85K milestone carries its own psychological weight: it's the first time since January that Bitcoin has reclaimed this level, suggesting either that the earlier capitulation washed out enough bears, or that fresh conviction has entered the market. Either way, the question now is whether this hold or whether it becomes another false peak. The liquidation data alone doesn't tell you which—it just tells you that, for now, shorts are paying the price.
