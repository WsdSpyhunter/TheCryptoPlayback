An Old Crypto Grudge Resurfaces Mid-Crash

As Bitcoin's slide reopened old wounds, OKX's Star Xu publicly blamed a large overseas crypto exchange for two straight years of October market crashes. It's a striking example of how crypto's biggest founders settle scores in public, turning a painful drawdown into a reignited personal feud rather than a quiet market retrospective.

Tom Lee Pins ETH's 'Waterfall' Drop on Nvidia and Anthropic, Bitmine Keeps Buying Anyway

Bitmine's ether stash fell to $11.8 billion from $14.3 billion a week earlier as ETH slid 28% in two weeks, a decline chairman Tom Lee blames on Nvidia fallout and cash-raising ahead of Anthropic's IPO. Despite the drop, Bitmine kept buying through the slump.

It's a tidy story: blame macro contagion, keep the conviction narrative intact, buy the dip on camera. Whether Nvidia and a single IPO can really explain a 28% two-week move is the kind of thing that's easy to assert and hard to prove, but the buying itself is the more verifiable fact here.

Nvidia Earnings Loom as Bitcoin's Next Wildcard

Bitcoin risks falling below $60,000 as Nvidia's upcoming earnings threaten to deepen the broader crypto sell-off, according to reporting on the market's current posture.

Crypto tying its fate to a single chipmaker's earnings call is a reminder of how tangled the AI trade and digital assets have become this cycle. Whatever Nvidia reports, it's worth remembering that correlation during a sell-off doesn't make Bitcoin an AI stock.

MEV Case Collapses: Peraire-Bueno Brothers Walk on Wire Fraud Charges

A judge acquitted the Peraire-Bueno brothers of wire fraud over the $25 million Ethereum MEV exploit, ruling that prosecutors showed no misrepresentation.

This was a closely watched test of whether exploiting a blockchain's own mechanics counts as fraud under existing law, and the acquittal suggests prosecutors' theory didn't hold up. It's a notable data point for anyone trying to figure out where the legal line sits between clever on-chain strategy and criminal deception.

Supreme Court Slams the Door on Custodia's Fed Account Bid

The Supreme Court said it would not take up Custodia Bank's challenge to secure a Federal Reserve master account, leaving a lower court ruling in place.

This effectively settles, for now, whether crypto-focused banks can force their way into the Fed's plumbing — they can't. It's a quiet but significant marker for how far 'regulatory clarity' actually extends when it comes to the banking system's front door.

Read more at The Block →

Germany Moves to Let Customs Freeze Crypto Without a Criminal Case

The Bundestag approved a bill that would let German customs freeze crypto and other assets over €100,000 of unclear origin without a criminal case, though permanent confiscation would still require a court. The bill now heads to the Bundesrat, with timing of that vote unclear.

Giving customs agents freeze power ahead of any criminal finding is a meaningful shift in burden, even with a judicial backstop on permanent seizure. Worth watching how this interacts with the EU's broader approach to crypto enforcement if it clears the Bundesrat.

Read more at Bitcoin Magazine →

Solana Drops Its 'Beta' Label After Six Years

The Solana Foundation retired the network's 'mainnet beta' label and introduced Solana 1.0 as a shared protocol version, giving developers a common way to track network rules.

Six years is a long time to carry a beta tag, and the symbolism matters more than the mechanics here — it's a statement that Solana wants to be treated as settled infrastructure, not a perpetual work in progress. Whether institutions treat it that way is a separate question from whether the label changes.

Read more at The Block →

Zelle Picks Solana for Its ZelleUSD Stablecoin

Zelle plans to use Solana for its ZelleUSD stablecoin and international payments, with confidential transfers designed to hide payment amounts.

A payments brand with Zelle's reach choosing Solana is a bigger adoption signal than most crypto-native stablecoin launches, simply because of who's already using the rails. The confidential-transfer piece is the detail worth tracking — privacy-by-default on a major consumer payment product would be a genuine first for mainstream fintech.

Read more at Cointelegraph →

Lighter Freezes Withdrawals After $10.4 Million Exploit

Lighter suspended all withdrawals after a $10.4 million crypto exploit, a reminder that security incidents don't pause just because the broader market is already under pressure.

Exploits during a drawdown hit differently — they compound the sense of fragility even when the vulnerability has nothing to do with price action. Platforms freezing withdrawals in response is standard incident response, but it's never a great look alongside an already shaky tape.
