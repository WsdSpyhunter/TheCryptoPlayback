Wall Street Keeps Tokenizing While Bitcoin Keeps Losing Its Moving Averages

Top story

Institutions keep racing to put stocks on blockchains even as bitcoin itself slides below a key technical level and gets dragged through a billion dollars of liquidations.

Here’s A Story You May Have Missed!

Vitalik Buterin and the Ethereum Foundation Get Sued Over a 'Suicide Pledge' Firing Dispute

A lawsuit against the Ethereum Foundation and Vitalik Buterin centers on a firing, alleging discrimination and retaliation, with the complaint citing a recorded conversation involving Buterin tied to a so-called 'suicide pledge' dispute. It's an unusually personal and strange legal entanglement for an organization normally known for protocol upgrades and research grants, not HR litigation.

Bitcoin Falls Below Its 50-Week Average as $1 Billion Gets Liquidated

Bitcoin closed below its 50-week moving average as more than $1 billion in crypto long positions were wiped out in 24 hours, a technical and sentiment gut-punch that arrived alongside a broader tech selloff tied to Nvidia jitters.

Losing a long-term trend line like this isn't the end of the world, but it's the kind of chart event that gets screenshotted a lot. The real story might be how quickly leveraged longs got flushed out — that's not a market being calm.

Read more at The Block →

NYSE Picks Avalanche to Settle Its Tokenized Stock Platform

The New York Stock Exchange tapped Avalanche to serve as the core ledger technology for the clearing firm behind its planned tokenized-securities venue, aiming for 24/7 trading, clearing and settlement of stocks onchain. AVAX jumped as much as 10% on the news.

When the exchange that's basically synonymous with Wall Street picks a blockchain for settlement, that's a genuinely different kind of validation than another retail trading app adding a token. Whether this actually reshapes how equities trade, or just becomes a very expensive pilot program, is the thing to watch from here.

Read more at Decrypt →

North Korean Hackers Launder Binance Loot Through Elon Musk and Cartoon Animal Memecoins

Blockchain analytics firm Elliptic says hackers tied to North Korea are creating their own throwaway memecoins — complete with Elon Musk and cartoon animal branding — and routing stolen funds from the Binance hack through homemade liquidity pools on NEAR to obscure the trail between wallets.

There's something almost darkly funny about state-sponsored hackers shipping their own shitcoins as money-laundering infrastructure, but it's a real reminder that on-chain tracing tools are good enough that criminals now have to build entire fake ecosystems just to slow investigators down, not stop them.

Read more at Decrypt →

Elizabeth Warren Sets Her Sights on Prediction Markets and the Trump Family's Crypto Empire

As she prepares to chair the Senate Banking Committee, Elizabeth Warren is signaling plans to scrutinize both prediction market oversight and the Trump family's expanding crypto ventures, after already pressing Kalshi and Polymarket on election trading.

Prediction markets and Trump-family crypto businesses have both grown fast with relatively light federal oversight, so it's not surprising someone's circling with a highlighter. Whether this amounts to actual legislation or mostly hearing-room theater is the part that matters for anyone operating in either space.

Read more at The Block →

Coinbase Finally Gets Its Full Federal Trust Charter

Coinbase's custody arm has completed its national trust bank charter with the OCC, upgrading an earlier conditional approval from April. The charter doesn't let Coinbase take deposits, make loans, or issue stablecoins, but it does put its institutional custody business under federal oversight.

This is the kind of regulatory plumbing story that doesn't move prices but quietly matters — institutions tend to care a lot about which regulator is standing behind a custodian, even when the headline doesn't grant any flashy new powers.

Read more at Bitcoin Magazine →

PNC Bank Starts Turning USDC Into Plain Old Dollar Deposits

PNC Bank is piloting a program, through its expanded partnership with Coinbase, that lets eligible corporate and institutional clients accept USDC payments on Ethereum, with each token converted to one US dollar before it lands as a deposit.

This is stablecoins doing the boring, useful thing they're actually good at: being a settlement rail that a traditional bank can bolt onto its existing deposit system without anyone involved needing to think about crypto at all.

Read more at Cointelegraph →

Vitalik Buterin Trades 'Ultrasound Money' for 'ETH Is Stability,' and Bitmine Takes the Hint

Ethereum co-founder Vitalik Buterin says ETH's value now comes from stability — decentralization, censorship resistance, and security — rather than the fee-burn narrative that defined 'ultrasound money.' Tom Lee's Bitmine responded by adding $20M in ether, pushing its stash to 6.07 million ETH, or 4.97% of supply, which Lee called a 'seismic shift.'

Watching Ethereum's own co-founder quietly retire a years-old meme is a bigger cultural moment than it looks like on the surface, and Bitmine treating it as a buy signal says a lot about how closely treasury firms are now watching Vitalik's Twitter feed for guidance.

Read more at Decrypt →

US Regulators Quietly Kill the $250 Travel Rule Plan — Crypto Language Included

FinCEN and the Federal Reserve formally withdrew a 2020 proposal that would have cut the cross-border funds-transfer reporting threshold from $3,000 to $250 and explicitly spelled out that the rule covers crypto. Any future version, regulators say, will need a fresh comment period.

A dormant, six-year-old proposal finally getting buried is a small win for anyone who didn't love the idea of $250 crypto transfers needing full travel-rule paperwork, though 'needs a fresh comment period' is bureaucratic for 'this isn't actually dead forever.'
