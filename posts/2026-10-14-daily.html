Quantum Fears, Hardware Hacks, and a Whale Wakes Up

Top story

Bitcoin is hovering near $83-84K on inflation jitters while the real drama today is happening off the price charts — in hardware wallets, quantum roadmaps, and one very patient whale.

Here’s A Story You May Have Missed!

The Whale That Slept Through Every Crash Since 2020 Just Woke Up

A wallet that received 4,581 BTC back in March 2020 — worth about $40 million at the time — finally moved its stash today, now worth roughly $377 million. That's a position that sat untouched through every crash, rally, and news cycle for over five years before finally stirring.
Read more at Decrypt →

Ledger Confirms Spy Implant in Reseller Hardware Wallets, MediaMarkt Pulls Devices

Ledger confirmed that devices sold through a reseller called CryptoBilis contained a hardware implant designed to steal users' recovery phrases, prompting MediaMarkt to suspend sales across Europe while the investigation continues. Trezor, not wanting to be caught flat-footed, has already started trimming its own reseller network as a precaution.

This is the nightmare scenario for the 'not your keys, not your coins' crowd — the compromise wasn't in the software or the seed phrase generation, it was in the supply chain before the device even reached a customer's hands. If you've ever bought a hardware wallet from anywhere other than the manufacturer directly, this is a good week to double-check where it came from.

Read more at The Block →

Coinbase Says the Quantum Clock Is Already Ticking for Bitcoin

Coinbase, which holds 5% of all bitcoin in existence, published its priorities for Bitcoin's post-quantum migration, pushing for flexible signature designs and urging the ecosystem to start preparing for fund migration now rather than waiting for quantum computers to actually arrive.

It's a classic 'the time to fix the roof is when the sun is shining' argument, and coming from an exchange sitting on that much BTC, it carries some weight. Whether the broader Bitcoin community moves with any urgency on a threat that's still theoretical is another question entirely.

Read more at CoinDesk →

Strive Drops $280 Million on Bitcoin, Its Biggest Buy Since January

Strive added 3,311 BTC between October 5 and 9, spending roughly $280 million to push its total holdings to 32,773 BTC — closing the gap with MARA in the process. Notably, the purchase came during a rough stretch for the market, not a euphoric one.

Corporate treasuries buying into weakness rather than chasing green candles says something about conviction, or at least about mandate. Either way, it's a reminder that the 'bitcoin treasury company' playbook is still very much being run, even when the charts aren't cooperating.

Read more at The Block →

Standard Chartered Opens Direct Bitcoin and Ether Trading to UAE's Wealthy

Standard Chartered expanded its spot crypto trading services to professional investors in the UAE, giving affluent clients direct access to bitcoin and ether through a traditional banking relationship rather than a crypto-native exchange.

A global systemically important bank quietly building out crypto rails for its wealthiest clients is the kind of institutional creep that doesn't make for flashy headlines but matters more than most token launches. This is what adoption actually looks like when it stops being a press release and starts being a product feature.

Read more at Cointelegraph →

France Floats a 31.4% Stablecoin Tax That Could Backfire Spectacularly

A proposed French tax of 31.4% on regulated stablecoins is raising concerns that it would simply push crypto traders offshore rather than capture the revenue regulators are hoping for.

This is the kind of policy that reads great in a budget meeting and terrible in practice — tax something mobile too aggressively and it just moves, taking the tax base and the oversight with it. If MiCA compliance becomes more expensive than non-compliance, don't be shocked when liquidity votes with its feet.

Read more at CoinDesk →

Bitcoin Climbs Toward $84K as Fed's Williams Rules Out 2027 Rate Hikes

Bitcoin pushed toward $84,000 after New York Fed President John Williams signaled he doesn't see rate hikes coming in 2027, giving risk assets a modest tailwind heading into fresh U.S. inflation data.

Macro is still writing most of bitcoin's short-term script, which is a little ironic for an asset that was supposed to exist outside the Fed's orbit. For now, 'number go up on dovish Fed speak' remains the dominant pattern, inflation data notwithstanding.

Read more at CoinDesk →

Evernorth's $648 Million XRP Treasury Gets Put to Work

Fresh off its Nasdaq debut, Evernorth said it's tapping Ripple, Kraken, Coinbase, and BitGo to begin deploying its $648 million XRP treasury into lending and yield strategies in a phased rollout.

Watching a public company's treasury strategy unfold in real time, with multiple major crypto players named as partners, is a sign of how normalized 'corporate crypto treasury' has become beyond bitcoin alone. Whether XRP-denominated yield strategies hold up as well as the headline implies is something only time — and a few quarterly filings — will tell.

Read more at The Block →

Binance Shuts Down Its Bitcoin Mining Pool After Six Years

Binance is discontinuing Binance Pool, Pool Savings, Cloud Mining, and other related services, retreating entirely from mining infrastructure after six years in the business.

Exchanges winding down mining operations isn't necessarily a bearish signal on bitcoin itself — it might just mean the mining infrastructure business has gotten too competitive or too regulatorily complicated to be worth the overhead for a platform better known for trading. Still, it's one more data point in the slow unbundling of what 'being a crypto exchange' even means.
