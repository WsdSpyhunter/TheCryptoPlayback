Breakouts, Buyouts, and a Broken Clarity Act

TOP STORY

Bitcoin is riding a macro tailwind while Washington's crypto bill dies, Binance writes a nine-figure check to its stablecoin rival, and the industry's prediction-market obsession keeps getting bigger numbers attached to it.

Bitcoin Rides the Everything-Rally as Oil Slides and Nasdaq Hits Records

Bitcoin is breaking out alongside a record Nasdaq and falling oil prices, with traders reading the combination — plus a Fed that hiked while quietly adding liquidity — as a green light for risk assets broadly.

It's a good reminder that bitcoin's 'digital gold' and 'risk-on tech stock' identities aren't mutually exclusive, they just take turns depending on which narrative is convenient that week. Enjoy the tailwind while it lasts; macro correlations have a way of reversing exactly when you've gotten used to them.

Bitcoin ETFs Pull Nearly $1 Billion in a Single Day

US spot bitcoin ETFs took in almost $1 billion on Monday alone — more than the entire prior week combined, which had just posted the weakest net inflow in the funds' history. The average holder is reportedly back in profit too.

Whiplash is the operative word here: a week of outflows followed by one of the biggest single-day inflows on record shows just how twitchy ETF demand has become. It says more about sentiment swings than about any underlying shift in conviction.

Read more at Decrypt →

Binance Buys $100M Stake in Circle, Locks In Five-Year USDC Deal

Binance and Circle closed an equity placement and a commercial partnership on the same day: Binance now owns a $100 million stake in Circle and has agreed to promote USDC on its platform for five years, with money flowing in both directions.

It's a notable détente between two entities that have spent years as stablecoin rivals more than allies. When the world's largest exchange puts equity behind a competitor's stablecoin, it's less about ideology and more about which token generates the most fee revenue for both sides.

Binance Under Federal Investigation Over Possible Sanctions Violations

US federal prosecutors are reportedly probing whether Binance knowingly allowed Iran-linked trades through its platform, testing whether the compliance overhaul the exchange implemented in 2023 actually held up. Binance says it maintains a zero-tolerance policy on sanctions violations.

Binance has already paid dearly once for compliance failures; a fresh federal probe, even one that ends in nothing, is a reminder that the exchange's past settlement bought a reprieve, not immunity. Regulators clearly aren't done watching.

Crypto's Super PAC Puts $30 Million Behind Unseating a Crypto Skeptic

Days after the Senate blocked the industry's Clarity Act, crypto super PAC Fairshake is committing $30 million to an Ohio race against Sherrod Brown, who has been openly skeptical of the industry.

Legislative failure, meet electoral retaliation. Crypto's political apparatus has clearly decided that if it can't win the argument in Congress this cycle, it can try to change who's sitting in Congress next cycle — a strategy that's expensive, blunt, and not exactly subtle about it.

Bernstein Doubles Down, Now Sees a $10 Trillion Prediction Market by 2035

Bernstein has revised its own forecast upward tenfold — from $1 trillion by 2030, a call it made just five months ago, to $10 trillion annually by 2035 for prediction markets.

Revising your own five-month-old forecast by 10x is either a sign of a genuinely fast-moving category or a sign that nobody actually has a clue how big this gets. Probably both. Big numbers make for great headlines regardless of how the math ages.

Zcash Gets Its First European ETP as Privacy Coins Go Mainstream(ish)

21Shares has listed physically-backed Zcash and EtherFi products on Euronext Paris and Amsterdam, following the earlier US ETF launch, as issuers continue building out access points for privacy-focused assets.

A privacy coin getting a regulated European wrapper is a small but genuine sign that 'private' and 'compliant' aren't automatically opposites anymore. Whether demand actually shows up for a wrapped privacy asset is a separate question entirely.

Canada's Biggest Banks Team Up on Tokenized Deposits

Canada's Big Six banks are launching a joint interbank tokenized deposit initiative, joining a growing list of national banking systems experimenting with blockchain rails for settlement between institutions.

Tokenized deposits are basically banks admitting blockchain settlement is more efficient without ever having to say the words 'we were wrong about crypto.' It's institutional adoption wearing a very conservative suit.

European Central Banks Want to Extend the Stablecoin Yield Ban to Lending and Staking

European central banks are pushing to expand MiCA's existing stablecoin yield ban so it also covers crypto lending and staking products, tightening the regulatory net around anything that looks like yield on digital assets.

Europe's regulators have been consistent on one point: they really don't want stablecoins competing with bank deposits for yield-seeking money. Extending that logic to lending and staking suggests the goal isn't just stablecoin-specific caution — it's a broader defense of the traditional banking system's turf.

Read more at CoinDesk →

White Hats Beat Hackers in a Race to Save 52 Bitcoin From a Coldcard Exploit

Following a Coldcard hardware wallet exploit, white-hat responders managed to secure roughly 40% of the bitcoin moved in the attack's second wave, transferring it to safety before hackers could claim it.

A real-time footrace between white hats and thieves over hardware-wallet vulnerabilities is a pretty vivid reminder that 'cold storage' doesn't mean 'unhackable' — it just raises the degree of difficulty. Securing 40% is a win worth noting, but it also means 60% wasn't.
