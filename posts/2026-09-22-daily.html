DAILY ISSUE

Billions Flow Back In, Regulators Flex, Hackers Get Outrun

TOP STORY

Bitcoin clawed back toward $86K and ETF money came roaring back, but the day's real story was how much is happening around the coin — from PAC wars to sanctions probes to a literal race against hackers.

Bitcoin ETFs Just Had Their Best Day in 11 Months

US spot bitcoin ETFs pulled in nearly $1 billion on Monday — the largest single-day inflow since October 2025 and a sharp reversal from what had been the weakest week in the funds' history. The average ETF holder is reportedly back in profit as bitcoin found footing near $86,000.

Nothing says 'crypto sentiment on a hair trigger' quite like going from worst-week-ever to ninth-largest-inflow-ever in the span of a few trading days. It's a good reminder that ETF flow charts are mood rings, not fundamentals.

Crypto's Favorite PAC Puts $30 Million Behind Beating a Senator

Days after the Senate blocked the industry-backed Clarity Act, crypto super PAC Fairshake is committing $30 million to an Ohio race against former Senator Sherrod Brown, a longtime crypto skeptic.

The industry has spent years insisting it just wants 'clear rules,' but $30 million aimed at a single race makes clear the actual goal is winning elections, not winning arguments. Whether that buys clarity or just louder ads is a separate question.

Read more at Decrypt →

Binance Buys Into Circle, Because Why Not Own Your Rails Too

Binance is taking a $100 million equity stake in Circle alongside a five-year deal to promote USDC on its platform, with money and incentives flowing both directions on the same day the deal closed.

Stablecoin issuers paying exchanges to push their coin, and exchanges taking equity in the issuer — it's less a partnership than a mutual insurance policy against each other's relevance fading. Efficient, sure, but it does blur the line between 'neutral marketplace' and 'stakeholder.'

Read more at The Block →

Binance's Sanctions Compliance Gets Another Look From Federal Prosecutors

Bloomberg reports US federal prosecutors are probing whether Binance knowingly allowed Iran-linked trades through its platform, testing whether the exchange's 2023 compliance overhaul actually held up. Binance maintains it has a zero-tolerance policy on sanctions violations.

Binance's 2023 settlement was supposed to be the closing chapter on this exact question, so a fresh probe suggests either old habits or old gaps. Either way, it's a reminder that a settlement isn't the same thing as a clean slate.

Read more at CoinDesk →

White Hats Won a Race Against Hackers Over a Coldcard Exploit

Following a Coldcard hardware wallet exploit, white hat responders managed to secure about 40% of the bitcoin moved in the attack's second wave, sweeping roughly 52 BTC into a recovery trust before attackers could get away with it.

A real-time footrace to rescue funds is about as close as crypto gets to a heist movie, and it's genuinely encouraging that the good guys won a meaningful share of this round. It's also a reminder that hardware wallets are only as secure as the software and supply chain behind them.

Read more at Cointelegraph →

European Central Banks Want to Choke Off Stablecoin Yield Entirely

The ECB and EU central banks are pushing to extend MiCA's ban on stablecoin yield beyond issuers themselves, targeting crypto lending and staking products that offer a similar return, on top of separately seeking changes to MiCA's minimum bank deposit requirements for stablecoins.

Regulators drawing a wide circle around anything that smells like stablecoin yield suggests they're less worried about specific products and more worried about stablecoins quietly becoming a shadow banking system. Fair concern, though it also means Europe's stablecoin rulebook keeps getting rewritten before it's even settled.

Read more at CoinDesk →

Coinbase Lets US Retail Traders In on IPO Allocations, Starting With Oura

Coinbase is opening IPO share allocations to eligible US retail customers, starting with wearable-maker Oura, letting users request shares at the offering price (though allocations aren't guaranteed). It extends Coinbase's push beyond secondary-market stock trading into the primary IPO market.

A crypto exchange becoming a retail on-ramp for traditional IPOs is a strange-but-logical pivot — it's Coinbase betting its userbase wants exposure to more than just tokens. Worth remembering that 'access' to an IPO and actually getting a meaningful allocation are two very different things.

Dogecoin Rallies Again, Elon Variable Included

Dogecoin hit its highest price in months, and once again Elon Musk's fingerprints appear to be somewhere on the move, according to Decrypt.

At this point Dogecoin's price chart is basically a Musk mood tracker with extra steps. It's a fun reminder that not every crypto rally needs a fundamental catalyst — sometimes a meme just wants to meme.
