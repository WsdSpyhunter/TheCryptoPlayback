Oil Drops, Bitcoin Pops, Binance Gets a Subpoena

TOP STORY

Bitcoin is riding a macro tailwind back toward $86K while regulators, PACs, and white-hat hackers all had unusually busy days behind the scenes.

Bitcoin Rides the Nasdaq's Coattails as Oil Slides

Bitcoin broke out alongside a record-setting Nasdaq as oil prices slid on hopes for an Iran de-escalation, with a Fed that hiked rates while quietly adding liquidity apparently keeping risk appetite alive.

It's a good reminder that Bitcoin's 'digital gold' narrative gets conveniently shelved whenever it's more useful to trade like a leveraged tech stock. Macro correlation isn't a flaw, it's just the current regime — enjoy it while stocks are hitting records, because that relationship cuts both ways.

Bitcoin ETFs Just Had Their Best Day in Nearly a Year

Spot Bitcoin ETFs pulled in roughly $999 million on Monday, the largest single-day inflow in eleven months and the ninth-biggest on record, coming right after the funds posted their weakest weekly inflow ever.

Whiplash is the theme of the ETF era: institutional money can flee and return within days, which makes these funds a decent mood ring for Wall Street sentiment but a lousy way to judge Bitcoin's 'real' adoption curve.

Read more at The Block →

Binance Buys Into Circle Right Before Getting Investigated

Binance took a $100 million equity stake in Circle alongside a five-year deal to promote USDC on its platform, tying the world's largest exchange more tightly to the leading dollar stablecoin issuer.

The timing is almost cinematic — a landmark stablecoin partnership landing the same week federal prosecutors are reportedly probing Binance over sanctions compliance. Two very different Binance headlines, one very busy PR department.

Read more at The Block →

US Prosecutors Ask If Binance Knowingly Waved Through Iran Trades

A Bloomberg report says federal prosecutors are investigating whether Binance knowingly let Iran-linked trades slip through, testing whether the exchange's 2023 compliance overhaul actually stuck. Binance maintains it has a zero-tolerance policy on sanctions violations.

Binance has been down this road before with a costly settlement, so this isn't uncharted territory. But 'we already paid for this once' isn't the legal defense it sounds like, and a fresh federal investigation is never just background noise.

Read more at Decrypt →

Crypto's Super PAC Declares War on Sherrod Brown After Clarity Act Defeat

Days after the Senate blocked the industry's Clarity Act, crypto super PAC Fairshake is committing $30 million to an Ohio race targeting former Senator Sherrod Brown, a longtime crypto skeptic.

This is the industry's actual regulatory strategy laid bare: if you can't win the vote, help pick the voters. Whatever you think of crypto policy, it's worth watching how much of this election cycle gets shaped by a single-issue PAC with a nine-figure war chest.

Read more at Decrypt →

European Central Banks Want an Even Tighter Leash on Stablecoin Yield

The ECB and other EU central banks are pushing to extend the existing stablecoin yield ban to cover crypto lending and staking as well, on top of separately seeking changes to MiCA's minimum bank deposit requirements for stablecoin issuers.

Europe keeps signaling that it wants stablecoins to behave like boring, yield-free payment rails rather than yield-bearing products — a philosophy that puts it on a collision course with how much of the rest of the industry, and its users, actually wants to use them.

Read more at CoinDesk →

Bernstein Doubles Down (Again) on Prediction Markets, Now Calling for $10 Trillion

Bernstein now projects prediction markets will handle $10 trillion annually by 2035 — ten times its own $1 trillion-by-2030 forecast made just five months earlier — with financial assets expected to overtake sports as the dominant category.

Revising your own five-month-old forecast tenfold isn't exactly a confidence-inspiring look for 'analysis,' but it does capture how fast this corner of crypto is moving from novelty to institutional talking point. Take the specific number with a grain of salt; take the direction of travel more seriously.

Read more at Decrypt →

White Hats Win the Race Against Coldcard Hackers

In the second wave of an exploit targeting Coldcard hardware wallets, white-hat responders managed to secure about 40% of the roughly 52 Bitcoin in motion, transferring it to safety before attackers could grab it.

A genuinely good-news security story is rare enough to savor, but it's also a reminder that hardware wallets — the thing crypto people recommend as the 'safe' option — are not immune to exploitation. Self-custody comes with self-responsibility.

Read more at Cointelegraph →

Canada's Biggest Banks Team Up on Tokenized Deposits

Canada's 'Big Six' banks are launching an interbank tokenized deposit initiative, bringing blockchain-based settlement into the country's mainstream banking plumbing.

This is the quieter, less flashy version of crypto adoption — no memes, no ETFs, just banks deciding tokenization is useful infrastructure rather than a threat. It's arguably more consequential long-term than most of the headlines that get more attention.
