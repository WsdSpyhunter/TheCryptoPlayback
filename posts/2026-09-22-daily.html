Clarity Dies, Money Doesn't Care

TOP STORY

Bitcoin is flirting with $86K, ETFs just had their best day in nearly a year, and Washington's crypto bill collapsed anyway — so naturally the market shrugged and kept buying.

Spot Bitcoin ETFs Just Had Their Best Day in 11 Months

US spot bitcoin ETFs pulled in nearly $1 billion on Monday — the ninth-largest single-day inflow ever, and a sharp reversal from last week's record-weak showing. The average holder is reportedly back in profit territory.

Nothing says 'crypto is back' quite like the fastest possible sentiment U-turn — a week of outflows followed by a billion-dollar day feels less like conviction and more like everyone waiting for someone else to blink first.

Binance and Circle Are Now Business Partners, Not Just Frenemies

Binance took a $100 million equity stake in Circle alongside a new five-year deal to promote USDC on its platform, with money and incentives flowing both directions on the same day the arrangement closed.

The largest exchange in crypto quietly becoming a shareholder in the largest dollar stablecoin issuer says a lot about where the real money is these days — it's not in the token wars, it's in owning the plumbing.

Read more at The Block →

Binance's Circle Handshake Comes With a Federal Sanctions Probe Attached

Even as Binance cozies up to Circle, Bloomberg reports the exchange is being investigated by US federal prosecutors over whether it knowingly let Iran-linked trades pass through its platform after its 2023 compliance overhaul.

Binance says it has a 'zero-tolerance policy' for sanctions violations, which is the kind of phrase that sounds reassuring right up until prosecutors start asking pointed follow-up questions.

Read more at CoinDesk →

The Clarity Act Died, So a $30 Million War Chest Is Coming for Sherrod Brown

Days after the Senate blocked the crypto industry's Clarity Act, super PAC Fairshake announced $30 million in spending against Ohio's Sherrod Brown, a longtime crypto skeptic, as control of Congress heads toward the 2026 midterms.

The lesson the industry seems to have taken from a failed vote isn't 'compromise more,' it's 'spend more' — which tells you exactly how crypto now plans to get its regulatory wins, one expensive Senate race at a time.

Read more at Decrypt →

European Central Banks Want an Even Tighter Leash on Stablecoin Yield

The ECB and other EU central banks are pushing to expand the existing stablecoin yield ban to cover crypto lending and staking too, on top of separately seeking changes to MiCA's minimum bank deposit requirements for issuers.

Europe built a stablecoin rulebook and is already trying to rewrite it before the ink dries — a reminder that 'regulatory clarity' in crypto often just means the rules keep moving, only more slowly and with better paperwork.

Read more at CoinDesk →

White Hats Won the Coldcard Hardware Wallet Race

Following an exploit targeting Coldcard hardware wallets, white-hat responders managed to secure about 40% of the roughly 52 bitcoin that attackers were moving in the second wave, transferring it to safety before the hackers could get there first.

A four-in-ten save rate isn't a total victory, but in a live footrace against hackers for other people's hardware-wallet funds, it's the kind of result that makes self-custody feel simultaneously empowering and terrifying.

Read more at Cointelegraph →

SoFi Goes Live With Stablecoin Settlement on Mastercard's Rails

SoFi has launched live SoFiUSD stablecoin settlement over Mastercard's network, with the card program expected to top $25 billion in annualized volume.

This is the unglamorous version of crypto adoption — no charts, no memes, just a stablecoin quietly settling real card transactions at meaningful scale, which arguably matters more than most of what makes headlines.

Read more at The Block →

Coinbase Lets US Retail Traders Buy Into IPOs, Starting With Oura

Coinbase is opening IPO share allocations to eligible US retail customers, letting them request shares at the offering price with wearable-tech company Oura as the first listing — though allocations aren't guaranteed.

Crypto exchanges muscling into traditional IPO access is a neat bit of role reversal: for years crypto platforms tried to look more like brokerages, and now a brokerage-adjacent crypto exchange is trying to democratize the part of Wall Street that's historically kept retail out.

Read more at Decrypt →

Wall Street Now Thinks Prediction Markets Beat Sports Betting

Bernstein projects annual prediction market volume could hit $10 trillion by 2035, driven by financial-asset contracts overtaking sports betting as the category's main growth engine.

Ten trillion dollars is the kind of number analysts print when a trend is genuinely accelerating or when they just want a headline — with prediction markets, it's honestly a bit of both right now.
