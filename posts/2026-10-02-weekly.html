Uptober Delivers, Hackers Don't Take a Day Off

TOP STORY

Bitcoin is flirting with $87K and dominance is creeping back toward 60%, but this week's real story is how much is happening underneath the price chart — from a wave of exploits to regulators quietly building the rules Congress couldn't pass.

Bitcoin pushes toward $87K as 'Uptober' lives up to its name

Bitcoin broke through a wall of ask liquidity to hit new October highs near $87,800, triggering over $120 million in short liquidations along the way, while open interest jumped $2.3 billion as traders paid up for bullish positions. Spot bitcoin ETFs kicked off the month with $103 million in fresh inflows after logging $2.65 billion for all of September — their second-best month since October 2025.

Seasonality narratives are fun to repeat, but the funding-rate and open-interest data suggest this rally has real leverage behind it, not just vibes about a calendar month. That cuts both ways — it can accelerate a move up, and it can accelerate a move down just as fast.

SEC finally tries to answer: who can hold your crypto?

The SEC proposed a framework letting investment advisers and funds use state trust companies as custodians, and in some cases self-custody crypto outright — an attempt to replace years of regulatory ambiguity that had kept many advisers from offering digital assets to clients at all.

This is the unsexy, plumbing-level stuff that actually matters more than another price headline. Custody rules have been the quiet bottleneck holding back institutional crypto adoption for years, and a clear compliance path matters more to the next five years of this industry than any single token's chart.

Read more at The Block →

The CLARITY Act died in Congress. The rules showed up anyway.

Bitwise CIO Matt Hougan argued that the CLARITY Act's failure actually left crypto with fewer restrictions and faster-moving SEC action than the bill would have produced — a messier but arguably quicker path to regulatory clarity via agency rulemaking rather than legislation.

There's something almost darkly funny about an industry that spent years begging Congress for a law, only to watch the agencies route around the dead bill and deliver results anyway. Whether that's a win or just a different flavor of uncertainty probably depends on which administration is running the agencies next.

NEAR Intents hacked for $3.8M, then names its suspect and gives him a deadline

NEAR Intents froze its cross-chain swap service after a contract bug let an attacker drain roughly $3.8 million, promising to make every affected user whole. Days later, general manager Alex Shevchenko said the protocol had identified the hacker and issued a public 48-hour ultimatum.

'We have identified you, sir' is either the most confident or the most delusional thing you can say to an anonymous attacker who just drained your protocol — we'll find out which once the clock runs out. Either way, promising full user compensation before you've recovered the funds is a bold bet on your own treasury.

MetaMask pulls ETH out of Lido over a 'security incident' nobody's fully explaining

MetaMask is exiting its Ethereum validators from Lido following an infrastructure 'security incident,' saying it has found no immediate threat to user wallets — though the withdrawn ETH could take up to 45 days to return, and stakers may lose some rewards in the meantime.

'No funds at risk, but also please wait 45 days' is a sentence that will read very differently depending on how this story resolves. Staking infrastructure is supposed to be the boring, solved part of Ethereum — this week was a reminder that it isn't quite there yet.

Illinois blinks on its crypto tax after industry lawsuit

Illinois officials and crypto industry groups jointly asked a state court to push the state's 0.2% Digital Asset Tax from January 1 back to July 1, 2027, while a legal challenge against it continues to play out.

A six-month delay isn't a victory lap, but it is a reminder that state-level crypto policy is just as contestable as the federal fight everyone's watching — and sometimes a well-timed lawsuit buys more time than a lobbying campaign ever could.

Read more at Decrypt →

A $1 billion XRP treasury is about to list on Nasdaq

Shareholders approved Evernorth's merger with Armada Acquisition Corp. II, clearing the way for the Ripple-backed firm — described as the 'largest pure-play' XRP treasury — to begin trading October 8 while holding roughly 473 million XRP.

The corporate-treasury-as-crypto-proxy playbook keeps expanding well beyond bitcoin, and XRP is the latest token getting the public-company wrapper. Whether that structure ages well once the novelty fades is a question the market, not me, will answer.

Read more at Decrypt →

Lloyds and Visa quietly settle real money in USDC

Lloyds used USDC to settle $750,000 of payment obligations with Visa during a seven-day live cross-border pilot — a small-dollar test, but a real one, run by a major UK bank and a global payments giant rather than a crypto-native startup.

Stablecoins doing boring back-office settlement work for traditional finance is exactly the kind of adoption that doesn't trend on crypto Twitter but matters more than almost anything that does. No hype, no token launch — just plumbing getting rewired one pilot at a time.

Porsche's 'long haul' Web3 bet lasts under four years

Porsche is shutting down its Web3 project and Pioneers Circle community as trading activity in its 911 NFT collection has dried up, closing a chapter the automaker once framed as a long-term commitment.

Legacy brands piling into NFTs during the bull-market frenzy promised 'long hauls' that mostly turned out to be marketing cycles with better production values. This doesn't kill the idea of brands doing something genuinely useful onchain — it just confirms that a JPEG collection attached to a car company was never that thing.
