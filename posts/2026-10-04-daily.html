Sanctions, Settlements, and a Very Expensive Dinner

TOP STORY

From IMF cash landing in El Salvador to community banks suing federal regulators over crypto charters, the industry's fight for legitimacy is playing out on a dozen fronts at once.

Clarity Act Stalls, But Wall Street Keeps Dealing Anyway

The Clarity Act — crypto's best shot at a clean federal market-structure framework — has stalled in the Senate, but according to CoinDesk, bankers aren't pumping the brakes on crypto dealmaking just yet. M&A and capital markets activity is apparently proceeding on the assumption that clarity, eventually, arrives in some form.

It's a telling signal: when the people whose job is literally risk management decide to keep working the pipeline despite legislative limbo, it suggests confidence that crypto's institutional integration is now bigger than any one bill's timeline. Or it just means the fees are too good to wait around for Congress.

'We Have Identified You, Sir' — NEAR Intents Gets Its Money Back

Near Intents recovered the full roughly $3.8 million drained in Thursday's exploit after the team publicly identified the attacker and issued a 48-hour ultimatum to return the funds. The money came back in full.

This is becoming a recognizable genre of crypto drama: hack, doxx, deadline, refund. It's oddly effective, mostly because on-chain forensics have gotten good enough that 'identified you, sir' isn't a bluff anymore — it's a real threat.

Read more at Decrypt →

El Salvador Collects $138M From the IMF It Once Picked a Fight With

El Salvador received $138 million from the IMF after securing Bitcoin-related waivers, with the country continuing efforts to scale back the state's direct involvement in Bitcoin activity, per Cointelegraph.

There's a neat irony here: the country that made Bitcoin legal tender in defiance of IMF orthodoxy is now getting IMF money partly by walking some of that back. Pragmatism tends to win these standoffs eventually, on both sides.

Read more at Cointelegraph →

Chainalysis Used AI to Chase Down North Korea's Bitget Hackers

Chainalysis says it used in-house AI to trace the $387 million Bitget breach from September 24 across four blockchains, tying it to North Korea and pushing the country's 2026 crypto haul past $1 billion, per Decrypt.

That billion-dollar figure is the real headline. State-sponsored hacking has become a reliable line item in North Korea's budget, and the fact that tracing firms now need AI just to keep pace tells you how industrialized this has gotten on both sides of the chase.

Read more at Decrypt →

Japan Adds Garantex to Its Russia Sanctions List

Japan has added the exchange Garantex to its list of Russia-related sanctions over the Ukraine war, joining the US, EU, and other jurisdictions that had already blacklisted it, according to Cointelegraph.

Garantex keeps getting sanctioned by everyone except the places that actually let it operate — a reminder that global coordination on crypto sanctions enforcement still moves slower than the entities it's trying to shut down.

Read more at Cointelegraph →

Community Banks Sue the OCC Over Crypto Trust Charters

A community banking group has filed suit against the OCC, claiming the regulator overstepped its mandate in granting trust bank charters to crypto firms, Cointelegraph reports.

This is the quieter, less glamorous side of crypto's regulatory battles — traditional banks pushing back on what they see as regulators handing crypto companies a side door into the banking system. Expect more of these turf wars as the lines between 'bank' and 'crypto firm' keep blurring.

Read more at Cointelegraph →

Crypto Job Postings Triple, But Nobody's Applying

CoinDesk reports crypto job postings tripled to over 1,200 in September — but applications actually fell. Demand for talent is surging even as the pool of interested applicants shrinks.

That gap is worth sitting with. Either the industry's reputation among job-seekers hasn't caught up with its hiring budgets, or the skills gap in crypto-native roles is wider than headcount growth alone can fix.

Read more at CoinDesk →

BlackRock Previews What Tokenization Does to Your Portfolio

CoinDesk takes a look at how BlackRock's tokenization push could reshape everyday investment portfolios, offering an early glimpse of what that transition might look like in practice.

When the world's largest asset manager starts sketching out tokenized portfolios, it's less a novelty story and more a signal that the plumbing of traditional finance is quietly being rebuilt underneath people's existing accounts, whether they notice or not.

Read more at CoinDesk →

Trump Hosts Another Meme Coin Dinner, Timing Be Damned

The TRUMP meme coin project is inviting its top 185 holders to a gala dinner with the president on November 22 — just two weeks after ethics disputes tied to Trump's crypto interests reportedly helped stall the Clarity Act in the Senate, according to Decrypt.

The optics write themselves. A bill meant to give crypto regulatory clarity gets held up partly over conflict-of-interest concerns, and the response is... another dinner for top token holders. Subtlety was never really the point.
