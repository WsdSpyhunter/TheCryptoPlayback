Hacks, Lawsuits, and the Slow Grind Toward Rules

TOP STORY

Bitcoin is catching its breath near $84K while the real action today is in courtrooms, hot wallets, and Fed proposal drafts.

Bitget Loses $352M in an Hour, Points Finger at Pyongyang

Bitget confirmed a $352 million breach after a newly created wallet drained hot and cold reserves across multiple chains in under an hour. CEO Gracy Chen says the attack came via spoofed transfers rather than compromised private keys, and that a preliminary investigation found IP addresses matching VPN patterns associated with North Korean hacking operations. The exchange says user funds are safe and losses will be covered by its User Protection Fund.

'Spoofed transfers, not private keys' is a distinction that matters for engineers but not much for anyone who had funds sitting on Bitget at the wrong hour. At this point DPRK attribution on a major exchange hack is less a shocking revelation than a Tuesday, which says more about the state of exchange security than anything else.

KelpDAO Sues LayerZero Over the Year's Biggest Exploit

KelpDAO has sued LayerZero and its CEO Bryan Pellegrino over the April 18 rsETH exploit that drained $292 million, alleging LayerZero endorsed the bridge setup in writing before the attack. Pellegrino has publicly dismissed the lawsuit as meritless.

This is the DeFi version of 'you told me it was fine in an email.' Whichever side is right, expect this case to become the template for who's actually liable when a protocol's infrastructure partner signs off on something that later blows up spectacularly.

New York Tries to Kick Polymarket Out of the State

New York Attorney General Letitia James and Governor Kathy Hochul have sued Polymarket, alleging the prediction market is running an illegal gambling operation without a license and exposing New Yorkers to gambling harms. The state is asking a court to block Polymarket from operating there.

Prediction markets have spent the last couple years insisting they're 'information markets, not betting sites' loudly enough that regulators eventually stopped believing it. This lawsuit doesn't settle the philosophical question, but it does put a very concrete price tag on how New York feels about the distinction.

The Fed Finally Shows Its Hand on Stablecoin Rules

The Federal Reserve opened two proposals for comment implementing the GENIUS Act, setting capital requirements, a two-day redemption window, and new reserve standards for stablecoin issuers it supervises, plus an application process for banks that want in on issuance.

After a year of 'the GENIUS Act passed, now what,' this is the what. Two days to redeem sounds reasonable on paper, but it's also exactly the kind of detail that determines whether a stablecoin actually behaves like cash in a crisis or just like a very confident IOU.

Bitcoin ETFs Filled Their Own Hole

US spot Bitcoin ETFs have clawed back a $5.8 billion outflow gap, even as daily inflows have slowed — Thursday brought in $191 million, the third straight day of deceleration, capping a six-day streak worth $2.8 billion.

Slowing inflows after a big streak isn't a red flag by itself, it's just what a rally looks like when it stops sprinting and starts walking. The ETF flows have become their own weather report for sentiment, which is either reassuring or slightly unnerving depending on how much you trust the forecast.

Researchers Sketch a Zcash-Style Future for Private Bitcoin

A new 'Shielded Bitcoin' spec proposes hiding senders, receivers, and transaction amounts using zero-knowledge proofs and encrypted notes, modeled on Zcash's privacy design — and notably, without requiring a soft fork. The tricky part of how BTC actually enters and exits the shielded system is left for a future paper.

Bitcoin privacy proposals have a long, mostly theoretical history of showing up, generating excitement, and then quietly not shipping. Skipping the soft fork requirement is a genuinely clever way to lower the political barrier, but 'we'll figure out the entry and exit points later' is doing a lot of load-bearing work in that sentence.

Peirce's Parting Shot: End the KYC 'Panopticon'

In one of her final speeches as SEC commissioner, Hester Peirce argued that regulators' 'data haystacks' put the very people they're meant to protect at greater risk, pointing to recent KYC leaks that have exposed crypto holders to phishing and physical attacks.

It's a striking note to leave on, and hard to argue with the underlying logic: every database of who-owns-what-crypto is also a shopping list for criminals. Whether the next generation of regulators takes that seriously or just files it under 'nice speech' is a different question entirely.

Someone Tried to Sell Ondo Finance After Its Founder Died

Reports indicate that attempts were made to sell Ondo Finance following the death of founder Nathan Allman, an unsettling wrinkle in an already difficult story for a firm that has become one of the more prominent names in tokenized real-world assets.

Founder-led crypto companies carry a specific kind of fragility that traditional corporations mostly designed around decades ago. This is a grim reminder that succession planning isn't just a boardroom formality — in crypto, it can become a live question almost overnight.
