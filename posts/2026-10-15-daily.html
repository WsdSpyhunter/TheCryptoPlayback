Inflation Spooks Markets, Regulators Keep Building Rails

Top story

Hot inflation data torched the charts across crypto and equities alike, even as the slower-moving machinery of regulation and infrastructure kept grinding forward.

Here’s A Story You May Have Missed!

A Crypto Treasury Lost 400,000 SOL and Nobody's Saying Where It Went

SkyAI's Solana treasury shrank 70% in under a month, and roughly 400,000 SOL is reportedly unexplained. A major shareholder, Bastion Trading, is now pushing back on a tender offer, rejecting $3 a share and demanding $3.50. It's the kind of corporate-treasury mystery that makes you wonder what exactly happens to on-chain assets once they're sitting inside a traditional company's balance sheet.
Read more at The Block →

Bitcoin Gets Caught in the Inflation Crossfire

Bitcoin slipped toward $81,000 after hotter-than-expected inflation data pushed the odds of an October Fed rate hike from 18% to 29% in just a week. Analysts now expect bitcoin to stay range-bound until there's more clarity on the Fed's next move.

This is the market doing what it always does when macro uncertainty spikes: freezing up and waiting for someone else to make the first move. Bitcoin's 'digital gold' narrative gets tested every time a CPI print comes in hot, and so far it's still trading like a risk asset first, hedge second.

Read more at Decrypt →

USDC Just Became a Big Deal in Brussels

The European Banking Authority has designated Circle's USDC as the EU's first 'significant' e-money token, triggering new supervisory responsibilities starting November 12.

This is a quiet but meaningful milestone: it means USDC is now big enough, by regulatory definition, to warrant the kind of scrutiny usually reserved for systemically important financial products. Stablecoins graduating into 'too important to ignore' territory is exactly the kind of regulatory attention issuers both want and dread.

Read more at The Block →

White House Crypto Council Loses Its Chief, Again

Patrick Witt has stepped down as director of the White House Crypto Council, leaving a leadership gap and marking the fourth high-level departure from the administration's crypto policy apparatus.

Revolving-door leadership at the policy level is never a great look when the industry is desperate for durable, predictable rules. Every time there's a new face at the top, the clock on actual legislative progress resets a little.

Read more at Cointelegraph →

Q3 Crypto Hacks Approach $1.2 Billion

Exploits at a large overseas crypto exchange and at Liquid have pushed quarterly crypto hack losses toward $1.2 billion, with compromised suppliers, developer tools, and infrastructure components cited as common entry points.

The attack surface keeps shifting away from obvious smart contract bugs and toward the supply chain — the tools and vendors exchanges trust by default. That's a harder problem to audit your way out of, and it's not going away anytime soon.

Read more at Cointelegraph →

Russia's Largest Bank Gets the Green Light to Trade Bitcoin

Sberbank has won approval to trade bitcoin, now holding both custody and exchange status under Russia's new crypto law. The bank plans to bring bitcoin, ethereum, and USDT to apps used by 110 million retail clients.

It's a striking reminder that crypto adoption doesn't follow a single political script — a state-adjacent banking giant in a heavily sanctioned economy embracing bitcoin at scale is its own kind of signal, regardless of how you feel about the messenger.

Read more at Decrypt →

Ledger Wants to Turn Your Hardware Wallet Into a Phone

Ledger has unveiled Atom, an Android-based security device with Solana Mobile integration, AI-agent controls, and new tamper protections — a move that comes amid an ongoing investigation into modified wallets.

Folding a full smartphone layer into a hardware wallet is an ambitious bet that security and convenience don't have to be opposites. Whether users actually want to carry a second phone just to keep their keys safe is a different question entirely.

Read more at The Block →

Hex Founder Richard Heart Reportedly Detained in Istanbul

Turkish media are reporting that Hex founder Richard Heart has been arrested in Istanbul, though the news comes amid conflicting reports about the details of his detention.

Heart has long been a lightning rod in crypto circles, and any development in his legal saga tends to draw outsized attention. Until there's official confirmation, this is one to watch rather than one to draw conclusions from.

Read more at CoinDesk →

A Major Mining Pool Just Shut Its Doors

A bitcoin mining pool that once handled 2.3% of the network's total hashrate has shut down after its share of the network collapsed by 65%.

Mining pool consolidation and failure is a quieter story than price swings, but it matters: the health and distribution of hashrate is part of what keeps bitcoin's security model credible. One pool's exit is a data point worth tracking, not a crisis, but worth tracking all the same.

Read more at CoinDesk →

Hyperliquid's Founder Makes His Pitch: Regulate the Platforms, Not the Chain

Hyperliquid's founder has said he wants U.S. regulators to oversee trading platforms rather than the underlying blockchain itself, drawing a line between infrastructure and the businesses built on top of it.

It's a familiar argument from the DeFi world — regulate the point of access, not the protocol — but it's notable coming from one of the sector's fastest-growing players. Where regulators ultimately draw that line will shape a lot of what gets built next.
