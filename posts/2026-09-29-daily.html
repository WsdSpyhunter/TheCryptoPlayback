Bond Yields Spike, Bitcoin Shrugs, Regulators Keep Building the Plumbing

TOP STORY

Treasury yields are doing their best impression of a horror movie soundtrack, but between Coinbase's new derivatives stack, a Senate report on Tether, and Newsom's memecoin crackdown, the actual crypto news cycle had plenty going on regardless.

Coinbase Now Runs Its Own Derivatives Stack, Top to Bottom

The CFTC approved Coinbase's derivatives clearing organization, completing the exchange's full in-house derivatives infrastructure — meaning it can now list, broker, and clear fully collateralized products without leaning on outside partners.

This is the kind of story that doesn't trend on Crypto Twitter but matters more than most that do. Owning the whole stack means less counterparty risk, more control over margin and settlement, and a real signal that US regulators are willing to let exchanges build serious institutional plumbing rather than just tolerate it from offshore.

Bitcoin Bounces Back Above $84K While Wall Street Sweats Bonds

Bitcoin rebounded above $84,000 even as US Treasury yields stayed elevated, with spot ETFs pulling in a comparatively modest $30 million on the day.

Watching bitcoin hold its ground while bond markets throw a tantrum is becoming a familiar rhythm this cycle. Whether that's genuine decoupling or just a temporary mood, it's worth remembering that a good week doesn't erase how tightly correlated this market can still be to macro sentiment when it wants to be.

Read more at CoinDesk →

NEAR Intents Blocks $50M From the Bitget Hackers

NEAR Intents says it prevented $50 million tied to the Bitget hacker from being laundered through its cross-chain swap system, putting the protocol's permissionless design to an unexpected stress test.

There's a real philosophical fight buried in here about whether cross-chain infrastructure should actively police stolen funds or stay neutral rails. NEAR just picked a side, and it's going to keep getting compared to protocols that argue the opposite every time a nine-figure hack makes headlines.

Senate Democrats Say Tether Is Propping Up Iran's Shadow Banking

A new Senate investigation alleges USDT has become central to Iran's shadow banking network, with Tether's own disclosure that it helped freeze $550 million in Iran-linked USDT this year running alongside the report.

Tether pointing to its freezing track record as a defense is a bit like a bank citing its fraud department while also being named in a money-laundering probe — both things can be true at once. Expect this to feed directly into whatever stablecoin legislation is still being fought over in Washington.

Strategy Buys Another 1,665 Bitcoin, Now Owns ~4% of All BTC That Will Ever Exist

Strategy added 1,665 bitcoin for $143 million, pushing total holdings to 847,666 BTC — worth roughly $70 billion and equal to about 4% of the entire 21 million supply cap.

At this point Michael Saylor's company is less a bitcoin buyer than a permanent feature of the supply-and-demand equation. Whether that concentration is a strength or a risk depends entirely on which decade you're asking about.

Blockchain.com Wants a $500M IPO — At a Fraction of Its Old Peak

Blockchain.com is reportedly targeting a $500 million IPO at up to a $6 billion valuation, well below its 2022 peak, joining a wave of crypto companies testing whether capital markets have finally thawed.

A discounted valuation for an IPO isn't necessarily a bad sign — it might just mean the company, and the market, have gotten more realistic. Either way, it's another data point that public listings are back on the table for crypto firms after a long freeze.

Newsom Bans California Officials From Launching Memecoins, Eyes Trump

Gavin Newsom signed a bill banning public officials from launching memecoins, alongside other legislation aimed at clearer restitution rules for crypto scam victims — a move widely read as a shot at Trump.

Whatever you think of the politics, banning officials from launching tokens off the back of their own office seems like a fairly low bar for 'basic conflict-of-interest rule,' and it says something about the last few years that it needed to be written into law at all.

Vitalik's Next Act: A 'Cryptographic World Computer'

Vitalik Buterin has laid out a long-term plan for Ethereum in which the upcoming Hegota fork is the network's last 'normal' upgrade, with everything after built on recursive STARKs and formal verification.

It's a genuinely ambitious framing — moving from incremental protocol tweaks to something closer to mathematically verifiable infrastructure. Whether Ethereum's sprawling ecosystem of validators, developers and rollups can actually execute on a vision that abstract is a separate question from whether it's a compelling one.

Hester Peirce Leaves the SEC With Unfinished Business

After nearly a decade at the SEC, Commissioner Hester Peirce is finishing her last week at the agency, reflecting on what she still wanted to accomplish and noting there's simply no good time to leave.

Peirce spent years as crypto's most reliable ally inside a famously unfriendly agency, earning the 'Crypto Mom' nickname for a reason. Her exit leaves a real question mark over who inside the SEC will keep pushing for clearer rules once she's gone.

The Restaking Boom Quietly Ran Out of Steam

Top restaking protocols are reportedly barely turning a profit these days, a stark contrast to the frenzy that surrounded the sector when it launched.

Restaking was pitched as a way to squeeze more yield and security out of the same staked capital, and for a while the hype matched the ambition. Thin margins now are a useful reminder that 'new primitive' and 'sustainable business' are two very different claims, and crypto has a habit of conflating them.
