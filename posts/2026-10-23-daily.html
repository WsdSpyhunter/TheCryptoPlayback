Top story

Bitcoin spent the last two days chopping around $81K while the real action happened in ETF filings, stablecoin launches, and a fresh round of code-and-courtroom drama.

Here’s A Story You May Have Missed!

A Water Heater That Mines Bitcoin While It Heats Your Bath

Heatbit is selling an $899 water heater element called Bitwater that swaps a tank's resistance wire for 20 mining chips, claiming it uses the same electricity to heat the water it would have used anyway. It's a small, almost absurd example of bitcoin mining being folded directly into household appliances rather than sitting in a warehouse somewhere. Whether the economics actually work out for a homeowner is unclear, but as a proof of concept for repurposing waste heat, it's a fun one.

Franklin Templeton Gets the Green Light on Dividend-to-Bitcoin ETFs

Franklin Templeton, the $1.7 trillion asset manager, registered two Nasdaq-listed funds that run a 95/5 split between U.S. stocks and bitcoin, automatically plowing every dividend payout into more bitcoin.

It's a clever bit of financial plumbing — you get your boring stock exposure while a steady drip of dividends quietly stacks sats in the background. Whether investors actually want their income reinvested into a volatile asset rather than cash is a separate question, but the structure itself is a neat sign of how normalized bitcoin has become inside traditional fund wrappers.

Read more at Bitcoin Magazine →

AI Fears Spill Into Crypto as Bitcoin Consolidates After a 3% Drop

Bitcoin price volatility cooled as stock markets battled another day of resurgent AI fears during Friday's Asia trading session, following a 3% loss.

At this point 'AI fears' feels like the market's go-to excuse of the quarter, and crypto keeps getting dragged along for the ride even when nothing fundamentally changed about bitcoin itself overnight. It's a reminder that bitcoin's correlation to risk-off tech sentiment hasn't gone anywhere, decoupling narrative aside.

Read more at Cointelegraph →

Bitcoin ETFs Swing Back to $636 Million in Outflows

US spot Bitcoin ETFs saw $636 million in withdrawals, erasing the previous day's inflows, with BlackRock's IBIT leading Thursday's net outflows at $254.1 million while four other funds also saw redemptions.

Three days of inflows followed by a single-day reversal of this size is a tidy illustration of how choppy institutional appetite remains — these flows are not a one-way street, and the headline-grabbing inflow streaks tend to get erased just as fast as they build.

Read more at The Block →

HSBC Brings a Stablecoin to Millions of Everyday Users

HSBC is rolling out RedCoin wallets, its first Hong Kong dollar stablecoin, to over 3 million PayMe app users, with plans to support tokenized investment subscriptions and expand into institutional uses.

When a bank the size of HSBC puts a stablecoin directly into a mainstream payments app rather than keeping it in an institutional sandbox, it tells you where the real near-term adoption story is — not speculative trading, but quietly replacing the rails under everyday payments.

Read more at Cointelegraph →

Bitcoin Core Rewrites Its Bug Disclosure Rules for the AI Era

Bitcoin Core says keeping certain vulnerabilities secret no longer offers much protection now that AI-assisted bug hunting makes code flaws dangerously easy to find.

This is the kind of unglamorous security housekeeping that rarely trends but genuinely matters — if AI tools can surface exploitable bugs faster than developers can quietly patch them, the entire responsible-disclosure playbook needs rewriting, and it's good to see Core moving ahead of the problem rather than reacting to it.

Read more at CoinDesk →

Ethereum Hits the Brakes on Glamsterdam Over Gas Bugs

Ethereum delayed the Glamsterdam Hoodi testnet fork after ChainSecurity identified gas changes that could let contracts lock in failures, citing 13 real-world cases of potentially affected smart contracts.

Pausing a fork because auditors found a way it could brick live contracts is exactly the kind of caution that should be boring and routine, yet it always triggers a wave of hand-wringing about Ethereum's development pace. Better a delayed testnet than a mainnet incident.

Read more at Decrypt →

Circle Shareholder Sues Trump Over $2B UAE Crypto Deals

A shareholder of stablecoin giant Circle has filed suit alleging the president corruptly cashed in on $2 billion worth of UAE crypto deals, claiming unconstitutional profits.

Lawsuits like this tend to move slowly and rarely land the way the filing suggests they will, but the optics of a sitting president's crypto dealings being challenged by a shareholder of a major stablecoin issuer are not nothing — it keeps the industry's entanglement with political power squarely in the headlines.

Read more at Bitcoin Magazine →

China Moves to Seize $5 Billion in Bitcoin From a Fled Fraudster

China is moving to confiscate $5 billion in bitcoin from an alleged fraudster who fled to the UK after trying to run away with nearly 62,000 bitcoins, according to a report.

Whatever you think of the underlying case, the sheer size of this stash is a reminder that some of the largest bitcoin holdings in the world sit not in ETFs or cold wallets of long-term believers, but in the hands of fraud suspects now entangled in cross-border legal fights.
