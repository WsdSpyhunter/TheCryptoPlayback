Banks Go On-Chain While Red Candles Keep Coming

Top story

Wall Street keeps planting flags on public blockchains even as ETF outflows, stablecoin wobbles, and a few ugly hacks remind everyone that crypto's plumbing is still very much under construction.

Here’s A Story You May Have Missed!

A 2013 Wallet Wakes Up

A Bitcoin whale moved $34 million worth of BTC that had been sitting untouched since 2013, back when coins traded at just $131 apiece. It's a quiet little reminder of how absurd the price trajectory has been for anyone who bought early and simply did nothing for over a decade. No drama, no hack, just a very patient wallet finally stirring.
Read more at CoinDesk →

JPMorgan Brings Its Deposit Token to Ethereum

JPMorgan is expanding its JPM Coin deposit token to Ethereum, letting institutional clients make peer-to-peer transfers and settle tokenized asset transactions within the Ethereum Virtual Machine ecosystem, including Base.

When one of the world's biggest banks puts its own dollar-deposit token on the same rails as memecoins and DeFi protocols, it's a pretty loud statement that 'blockchain rails' and 'crypto' are quietly becoming two different conversations.

Read more at The Block →

USD1 Stablecoin Bleeds $400 Million in Two Weeks

World Liberty Financial's USD1 stablecoin has seen its market cap drop more than $400 million over two weeks, driven by large redemptions on Ethereum and BNB Chain.

Stablecoins are supposed to be the boring, stable part of this industry, so a quarter of a billion dollars walking out the door that fast is the kind of thing worth watching even if nobody's panicking yet.

Read more at Cointelegraph →

Bitcoin and Ether ETFs Keep Shedding Money

US spot Bitcoin and Ether ETFs shed a combined $549 million recently, with Ether funds alone recording nearly $1 billion in October net outflows and Bitcoin funds now two weeks into a withdrawal streak.

'Uptober' is turning into a bit of a misnomer this year, and ETF flows are a decent reminder that institutional interest can reverse just as fast as it arrived.

Read more at Cointelegraph →

Qubic Freezes Its Own Chain After Weak-Seed Wallet Drain

Qubic froze its network after an attacker drained 1,400 wallets secured by weak seeds, with the project saying 87.1% of stolen funds remain onchain while the rest were frozen at an exchange as validators prepare to vote on a recovery plan.

Freezing a chain to recover funds is a dramatic intervention, and it always reopens the old debate about how much 'decentralization' survives the moment things actually go wrong.

Read more at Cointelegraph →

Zcash Devs Race to Quantum-Proof Shielded Transactions

Zcash developer Zakura has proposed a fix to stop future quantum computers from linking shielded swaps, warning that a single published Zcash address could let an attacker eventually trace every payment a wallet has ever received.

Quantum computing is still mostly theoretical as a threat, but privacy protocols patching their defenses now is exactly the kind of unglamorous work that matters long before anyone notices it was needed.

Read more at Decrypt →

France Eyes Taxing Stablecoin Swaps and Self-Custody

France's National Assembly adopted a crypto tax amendment targeting certain stablecoin swaps, while separate proposals covering exit taxes and self-custodial wallet reporting remain under consideration.

Europe's MiCA framework was supposed to settle the regulatory questions, but individual member states clearly still have their own ideas about where to draw the tax line.

Read more at Cointelegraph →

South Korea Weighs Delaying Its Crypto Tax

South Korean finance officials are reportedly set to meet major crypto exchanges, days after a lawmaker introduced legislation that could push the country's crypto tax start date back by two years.

Tax delays like this tend to say as much about political pressure from an engaged trading public as they do about policy readiness.

Read more at Cointelegraph →

World Takes 'Proof of Human' to Zoom Calls and Banks

Sam Altman-backed World has rolled out biometric 'proof of human' badges for Zoom enterprise users to combat AI deepfake impersonation, while also pitching a proof-of-human ID suite to banks citing a $40 billion AI fraud risk, with design partners including Bain Capital, VanEck and Pantera Capital.

It's a strange sign of the times that proving you're a real human on a video call is now a feature banks and enterprises are willing to pay for.

Read more at Cointelegraph →

Crypto Vault Deposits Cross $10 Billion, And Regulators Notice

Crypto vault deposits have topped $10 billion, a milestone that's drawing regulatory scrutiny over the lending risks tied up in these yield-bearing products.

Big numbers attract big attention, and once regulators start circling a fast-growing corner of DeFi lending, the next chapter is usually about disclosure and risk limits rather than growth.

Read more at CoinDesk →

A Bitcoin Miner's AI Pivot Hits a Power Wall

Nscale, a Bitcoin mining offshoot that pivoted toward AI infrastructure, has lost a Microsoft AI deal as a power delay pushes timelines out into the 2030s.

Plenty of miners have rebranded themselves as AI data center plays, and this is a reminder that swapping ASICs for GPUs doesn't make the actual bottleneck — electricity — disappear.
