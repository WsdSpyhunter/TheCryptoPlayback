Clarity's Ghost, Bitcoin's Range, and a Fox Pulling Out of Lido

TOP STORY

Between a stalled Senate bill reshaping crypto's political strategy and Bitcoin stubbornly refusing to leave its summer trading range, the industry spent the day proving that 'boring' and 'eventful' can coexist.

Bitcoin Opens Q4 Right Where It Left Off

Bitcoin kicked off the new quarter still parked in the same $82,000-$85,000 band it's been trading in for weeks, even after a brief post-PCE pop that quickly faded as bond yields refused to cooperate.

There's something almost comic about an asset famous for violent moves settling into a rut this stubborn. Ranges like this tend to resolve eventually — the question is just which direction gets tired of waiting first.

Citi Bumps Its Bitcoin Target to $113K

Citigroup raised its 12-month bitcoin price target to $113,000, citing the recent return of ETF inflows after a quiet stretch.

Wall Street price targets are more weather vane than forecast — they tell you which way institutional sentiment is leaning, not what's actually going to happen. Still, a big bank getting more bullish while retail sentiment feels lukewarm is the kind of divergence worth noting.

Read more at CoinDesk →

Bitcoin ETFs Just Had Their Best Quarter in Years

US spot bitcoin ETFs pulled in $6.34 billion in Q3 as BTC gained nearly 43% — its best third-quarter performance since 2017.

It's easy to get numb to ETF flow headlines at this point, but a 43% quarterly gain alongside billions in fresh inflows is a reminder that the 'ETFs are boring now' narrative was premature. Boring wrappers, not-boring returns.

Read more at Cointelegraph →

MetaMask Pulls ETH From Lido Over a Mystery 'Security Incident'

MetaMask is exiting its Ethereum staking positions with Lido after flagging an undisclosed infrastructure security incident, though the wallet says there's no immediate threat to user funds — the catch is the withdrawn ETH could take up to 45 days to fully return.

'No immediate threat' paired with 'vague security incident' and 'six-week withdrawal window' is the kind of sentence that should make any DeFi user's eyebrow twitch, even when nothing ends up going wrong. Infrastructure risk doesn't announce itself politely.

Read more at CoinDesk →

EU Regulators Zero In on Binance's Loophole

European regulators are reportedly probing how Binance continues serving EU customers from Abu Dhabi via a 'reverse solicitation' exemption, months after the exchange lost its MiCA registrations in the bloc.

Reverse solicitation exemptions exist for a reason, but when an exchange that just lost its license keeps serving the same customers through a side door, regulators tend to notice the pattern. This feels like the opening move in a longer fight, not the conclusion.

Read more at Decrypt →

CFTC Wants Event Contracts Legally Defined as Swaps

The CFTC is pushing to formally classify event contracts as swaps, a move that would bolster its claim to exclusive federal jurisdiction over prediction markets as states separately sue operators over gambling concerns.

Prediction markets have become the industry's new jurisdictional battleground, and definitions matter enormously here — whoever controls the label controls the rulebook. Expect this fight to drag on well past any single ruling.

Read more at Cointelegraph →

September Was Crypto's Worst Month for Hacks This Year

Crypto hacks topped $768 million in September, the worst month of 2026, driven largely by the $388 million Bitget breach and a $320 million Liquid Network exploit.

Every time the market has a good quarter, security seems to have a bad one — it's almost a law of physics at this point. Bull markets attract both capital and criminals, and this September delivered a reminder that one tends to follow the other.

Read more at Cointelegraph →

After Clarity Act's Collapse, Crypto's Lobby Goes to the Ballot Box

The Coinbase-backed Stand With Crypto group has rolled out its first Senate midterm endorsements — Republicans Jon Husted and Ashley Hinson and Democrat Chris Pappas — just two weeks after the Clarity Act stalled in the Senate.

When legislation fails, the industry's playbook shifts from lobbying bill text to electing friendlier faces, which is a longer and messier game but arguably a more durable one. It's also a tacit admission that this fight isn't getting settled in this Congress.

Read more at Decrypt →

Dogecoin Gets a DeFi Layer, Memes Get Ambitious

The team behind the MyDoge wallet opened a public testnet for DogeOS, a zero-knowledge rollup layer that would let developers build lending platforms and games on top of the Dogecoin network, with miners eventually expected to help secure its apps.

Giving a meme coin a programmable app layer is either visionary or a solution looking for a problem — probably a bit of both. Plenty of chains have promised an ecosystem of builders; what matters is whether anyone actually shows up to build.
