Here’s A Story You May Have Missed!

A wrapped Monero token just evaporated 99% overnight

Wrapped Monero plunged 99% after a wallet linked to its issuer sold $13.5 million worth of the token. It's a stark illustration of how 'wrapped' versions of privacy coins carry a very different risk profile than the underlying asset itself — the wrapper's trust model can collapse even if Monero itself does nothing at all. A reminder that convenience products built on top of an asset are not the same thing as the asset.

Bitcoin ETFs bleed $582 million as November outflows near $3.5 billion

US spot bitcoin ETFs saw $582 million in net outflows on Thursday, with BlackRock's IBIT accounting for nearly all of it even as Morgan Stanley's MSBT pulled in $49.6 million. November's cumulative outflow total is now nearing $3.5 billion.

A single fund dominating the selling while a smaller product still attracts fresh money tells you institutional conviction isn't uniform right now — some allocators are clearly rotating, not fleeing outright.

Read more at The Block →

Bitcoin's death cross shows up right as Anthropic steals the market's attention

Anthropic shares surged on their Nasdaq debut, but broader stocks slid and bitcoin fell lower alongside them. Bitcoin's daily chart is now showing deeply oversold momentum, a strong downtrend, and fragile support, with the death cross pattern cited as part of the picture.

It's a strange juxtaposition — a blockbuster AI IPO should theoretically be a 'risk-on' signal, yet bitcoin couldn't catch the tailwind. That disconnect is worth sitting with.

Read more at Decrypt →

Binance hacker shuffles nearly $600 million in stolen ETH through THORChain and Chainflip

OnChain Lens tracked close to $600 million in ETH transfers over two days as the Binance hackers moved the funds through fresh wallets and cross-chain protocols in an apparent effort to obscure the trail.

Every time stolen funds this large move through decentralized bridges, it's a live stress test of whether cross-chain infrastructure can actually be used to launder at scale — and a reminder that 'decentralized' doesn't mean untraceable.

Read more at Cointelegraph →

MoonPay wants to become a bank

MoonPay has applied for a national trust bank charter to expand its custody and stablecoin services under federal oversight, joining a rush of crypto companies seeking similar charters amid growing political scrutiny.

Every crypto payments company chasing a bank charter is effectively betting that regulatory legitimacy is worth more than regulatory distance. Given how politically charged this space has become, that's not a small bet.

Read more at Decrypt →

VanEck drops Gemini, sticks with Coinbase for ETF custody

VanEck plans to end Gemini's custody agreements for three crypto ETFs in February 2027, while Coinbase will continue serving as custodian for the funds.

Custody consolidation like this quietly concentrates risk and influence in fewer hands — worth watching regardless of which side of the ETF business you're paying attention to.

Read more at Cointelegraph →

Senate votes to ban 'Death Bets' prediction markets

The Senate passed the Death Bets Act, which would ban prediction market contracts tied to death, terrorism, assassination, and war. The bill now heads to the House.

Prediction markets have been crypto's hottest regulatory battleground lately, and this is the first clear line Congress has drawn — not around the category itself, but around its most macabre edges.

Read more at The Block →

CFTC says onchain venues like Hyperliquid don't get a free pass

The CFTC said there will be no special rules for Hyperliquid or other onchain trading platforms — they'll need to follow the same framework as other exchanges to operate in the US.

This is the regulator choosing consistency over carve-outs, which is probably the least chaotic outcome for an industry that's spent years asking 'but what rules actually apply to us?'

Read more at CoinDesk →

Big institutions quietly bought $4 billion of bitcoin funds in Q3

After nine straight months of selling, big money bought a net $4 billion of bitcoin funds in the third quarter — a reversal that landed just as spot prices have been sliding since.

The timing is almost comic: the quarter institutions finally turned buyers is the same stretch that preceded this month's selloff. It's a useful reminder that institutional positioning and near-term price action don't always move in lockstep.

Read more at CoinDesk →

Ethereum's next big upgrade slips into 2027

Ethereum's Glamsterdam upgrade is being pushed into 2027 because proposed changes could break existing apps — developers have been reaching out to at least 50 affected companies to work through compatibility concerns.

Delays like this are the unglamorous tax of maintaining a network that real applications and real money actually depend on. Better a slower timeline than a breaking one.

Read more at CoinDesk →

Sky's stablecoin shrinks 35% after an illicit-finance report spooks holders

Sky's USDS stablecoin shrank 35% as $1.5 billion exited its reserves following a report on illicit finance concerns.

Stablecoins live and die on trust in their reserves, and this is what a confidence shock looks like in real time — fast, large, and driven entirely by perception rather than any single confirmed event.
