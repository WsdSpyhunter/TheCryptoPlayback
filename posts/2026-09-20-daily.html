Regulatory Void: Clarity Act's Failure Hands Control Back to SEC and CFTC

The Clarity Act didn't make it through the Senate, and that's actually worse for crypto than it sounds. The bill was supposed to establish a clear regulatory framework with the CFTC overseeing crypto commodities and the SEC handling securities—basically, crypto's chance at an explicit, legislated rulebook. Now that it's dead, the industry reverts to the regulatory regime it already knows: the SEC and CFTC making it up as they go, with zero legislative guardrails.

This matters enormously. Congress just handed the keys back to the agencies, which means expect more enforcement actions, more interpretation-based regulation, and more of the regulatory whack-a-mole that's defined crypto policy for the past five years. Kalshi and Coinbase can file for perpetual futures approvals, Bastion can get its bank charter, and leveraged ETFs can launch—but all of it exists in a legal gray zone where agency discretion trumps statutory clarity. The industry didn't lose the battle; it lost the chance to fundamentally change the game board.

What's particularly galling is the timing. Wall Street keeps productizing crypto—single-stock perps, leveraged Bitcoin ETFs, tokenized assets—but they're doing it under regulatory uncertainty that could evaporate overnight if an agency changes its mind or a court ruling reshuffles priorities. The Clarity Act would have at least codified some of this. Without it, every innovation is technically playing with fire.
