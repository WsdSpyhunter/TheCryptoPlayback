Clarity Act Flatlines in the Senate, and Guess Who's Celebrating

The Clarity Act died in the Senate, and CoinDesk's reporting suggests the real winners aren't US crypto firms but banks and offshore hubs like Dubai, which get to keep operating in the regulatory gray zone the bill would have erased.

This is the classic DC outcome: gridlock doesn't protect consumers, it just relocates the action somewhere with fewer cameras. Every year Congress fails to act is another year US firms watch business drift to jurisdictions that actually bothered to write the rules.

Bitcoin Blasts Through $85K on the Back of a Short Squeeze

Bitcoin topped $85,000 for the first time since January, and Decrypt reports the move started as a short squeeze that liquidated $648 million in bearish bets — but spot buyers have kept the momentum going since, with selling pressure near a record low.

Short squeezes are great theater but lousy foundations. The fact that spot demand seems to be picking up the baton afterward is the more interesting detail here; that's the part that actually tells you something about conviction rather than just leverage getting punished.

Read more at Decrypt →

Saylor's Strategy Gets Back in the Game

Strategy resumed buying after a two-week pause, adding 950 Bitcoin for roughly $76 million and pushing its total holdings within 0.2% of the June record, according to Decrypt. Holdings had bottomed at 840,447 BTC in August before climbing back up.

Watching Strategy's buy-pause-buy rhythm has become its own little market indicator, whether or not that's a fair thing to lean on. A two-week pause followed by a return to buying doesn't tell you much on its own, but it certainly makes for consistent headlines.

Read more at Decrypt →

Tom Lee Says Institutions Are Still Underweight Crypto

Bitmine is nearing its target of owning 5% of Ethereum's circulating supply after another 27,562 ETH buy, and Tom Lee is using the moment to argue the broader crypto bull market is underway and that institutions still haven't caught up, per The Block. Bitmine's total investments now stand at $17.1 billion.

'Institutions are underweight' is one of those lines that's been true for roughly a decade of crypto commentary, so it's worth treating as a permanent talking point rather than fresh news. That said, a single company approaching 5% of ETH's entire supply is the kind of concentration stat that deserves attention regardless of who's saying it.

Read more at The Block →

Visa Finally Closes the Meme Coin Rewards Loophole

With the Clarity Act dead, JPMorgan scored a narrower win elsewhere: Visa is moving to stop meme coin purchases from being miscoded as 'digital media' and quietly racking up card rewards points, according to Decrypt.

It's a small fix, but a telling one — the industry's biggest regulatory wins right now aren't sweeping legislation, they're plumbing patches like this one that quietly close off the weirder edges of crypto-finance overlap.

Read more at Decrypt →

X Sues Its Own Users Over an Alleged Bitcoin Bot Farm

X is suing a group of its own users, alleging they ran a coordinated fake Bitcoin news bot farm to inflate engagement and fraudulently extract creator payouts, per CoinDesk.

There's something almost poetic about a platform built partly on crypto hype now having to litigate against the very engagement farms that hype attracted. It's a reminder that 'crypto news' accounts on social media are, for some operators, less journalism and more arbitrage.

Read more at CoinDesk →

The ECB Builds Its Own Settlement Rail, No Stablecoins Required

The European Central Bank has deployed Pontes, a platform for settling wholesale tokenized assets directly in central-bank money, per CoinDesk — essentially tokenization infrastructure that deliberately routes around stablecoins entirely.

This is the quiet, unglamorous version of crypto adoption: central banks borrowing the plumbing without touching the tokens that made the plumbing famous in the first place. It's also a pointed statement about who Europe wants controlling settlement rails in a tokenized future — and it isn't Tether.
