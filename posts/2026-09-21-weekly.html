Bitcoin ripped to an eight-month high, Michael Saylor's buying hand twitched back to life, and Washington's crypto bill flopped just in time for the regulators to grab the wheel anyway.

Bitcoin Blasts Past $85K on the Back of a Short Squeeze

Bitcoin hit its first $85,000 print since January, and the move wasn't exactly organic — a wave of short liquidations (roughly $648 million worth) kicked things off before spot buyers took the baton and kept running. One measure of selling pressure is reportedly near a record low, which the optimists will read as a sign that the coast is clear.

Short squeezes make for great headlines and terrible foundations. The rally looks real enough that spot demand is doing some of the lifting too, but it's worth remembering that a market cleared out of shorts is a market that just lost its built-in skepticism — for better or worse.

Saylor's Strategy Gets 'A Little More Orange' Again

After a two-week pause, Strategy bought 950 more bitcoin for $76 million, pushing its total holdings to around 846,000 BTC — roughly 4% of the entire 21 million supply cap, worth about $72 billion. The company also repurchased $174 million of its STRC preferred stock in the same stretch.

Whatever else you think about the treasury-company model, Strategy's buying pattern has become a genuine market tell at this point — resuming purchases after a pause is the kind of signal traders parse for meaning, whether or not it deserves that much weight.

The Clarity Act Died in the Senate, So the SEC and CFTC Are Writing the Rules Instead

With the Clarity Act failing to advance, the industry's focus has shifted away from Congress entirely and toward a fresh wave of rulemaking from the SEC and CFTC, who aren't waiting around for lawmakers to sort themselves out.

This is the classic Washington move: when the legislative branch can't get its act together, the regulators quietly become the ones writing the rulebook. Crypto wanted statutory clarity from elected officials; instead it's getting agency-drafted clarity, which comes with its own flavor of uncertainty — namely, that the next administration can rewrite it just as easily.

The ECB Builds Its Own Tokenization Rails — No Stablecoins Required

The European Central Bank launched Pontes, a platform for settling tokenized assets directly in central-bank money, with plans to expand its services and operating hours gradually.

Notice the framing: settlement 'without stablecoins.' Central banks watching the private sector build stablecoin rails and deciding to build their own central-bank-money alternative is about as clear a signal as you'll get that Europe intends to keep tokenized finance firmly inside its own perimeter.

Grayscale's Zcash ETF Is Splitting Shares After a Blistering Month

Grayscale's red-hot Zcash ETF is doing a 3-for-1 forward share split to make itself more affordable after pulling in more than $233 million in under a month, as Wall Street piles into what's being called crypto's hottest privacy trade.

A privacy coin ETF gathering nearly a quarter-billion dollars in weeks is a strange and slightly funny plot twist for an industry that spent years being told privacy tech was regulatory poison. Apparently demand doesn't care much about the old narratives.

France's 'Wrench Attack' Problem Isn't Slowing Down

A crypto worker's children were reportedly held hostage in the latest French 'wrench attack,' with $46,000 taken. France remains the world's worst-hit country for these physical extortion schemes, with more than 70 logged in just the first eight months of 2026.

Self-custody has real upsides, but stories like this are the ugly flip side — when your wealth is a set of keys instead of a bank balance, the threat model shifts from hackers to actual people showing up at your door. Operational security isn't a nerdy afterthought here; it's the whole ballgame.

Read more at Decrypt →

Google and Apple Are Now Hunting for Crypto Talent

Big Tech is reportedly staffing up for stablecoins and tokenization, with Google and Apple both eyeing crypto talent as they eye the rails underlying digital payments and asset tokenization.

When the companies that build the phones and search engines everyone already uses start hiring for stablecoin rails, that's a bigger distribution story than any single token launch. The infrastructure fight is quietly moving from crypto-native firms to the platforms that already have billions of users.
