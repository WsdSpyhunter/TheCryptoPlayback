Congress dropped the ball on crypto clarity this week, so the SEC and CFTC picked it up and ran — while Bitcoin staged a short-squeeze comeback nobody had penciled in.

The Clarity Act Died, So the Regulators Took the Wheel

The Clarity Act failed to advance in the Senate, and rather than wait around for Congress to try again, the SEC and CFTC have started moving on their own. The industry's focus has shifted from legislative hope to a new wave of agency-level action.

This is the classic Washington move: when the legislature can't agree, the agencies fill the vacuum with rulemaking that's faster but far less durable. It's good news if you wanted movement, less good if you wanted certainty that survives the next administration change.

Bitcoin Shrugs Off the Clarity Act Wipeout, Reclaims $80,000

Just as traders braced for the Clarity Act's collapse to tank the market, Bitcoin instead reclaimed $80,000, with Solana and Hyperliquid joining the rally. The moves came even as the SEC and CFTC pressed ahead with crypto rulemaking despite Congress stalling.

There's something almost comedic about a market that spends a week pricing in doom over a bill nobody in DC could pass, only to rip higher the moment the bill officially dies. Markets hate uncertainty, apparently even more than they hate bad outcomes.

North Korean 'Recruiters' Infected 30,000 Devices Chasing Crypto Devs

The North Korean cyber group WaterPlum ran fake job offers at crypto, AI and NFT companies to lure developers, infecting 30,000 devices and stealing $10.7 million in crypto in the process.

The lesson here isn't new, but it never stops being relevant: if a job offer feels too good, too fast, or too eager to get you to run some code, that's not luck, that's reconnaissance. State-sponsored hacking crews have clearly figured out that developers are the softest target in the whole stack.

Polymarket's Growth-First Playbook Nearly Cost It $10 Million

A WSJ report says Polymarket faced a $10 million fraud attempt while its CEO pushed growth over compliance concerns. In a separate incident, hackers compromised nearly 500 user accounts using stolen personal information.

Prediction markets are having a genuine cultural moment, but moments like this are a reminder that 'move fast' and 'handle other people's money' don't always mix well. Compliance teams exist for a reason, even when they're annoying.

Coinbase, Robinhood, Circle Line Up for the Tokenized-Stock Land Grab

Analysts say Coinbase, Robinhood, and Circle could be early winners as the SEC pushes ahead on tokenized stocks, positioning these platforms to capture whatever wave of on-chain equities regulators are now clearing a path for.

Everyone's suddenly racing to put stocks on-chain, and the companies already fluent in both crypto rails and retail brokerage look like they've got a head start. Whether 'tokenized' actually changes anything for the average trader remains an open question worth watching, not betting on.

Zcash Devs Want Private Payments Three Times Faster

A proposed upgrade would cut Zcash's block time from 75 seconds to 25, while also quietly rewriting how the network funds its own security after 2031.

It's a good sign for a chain when the roadmap conversation shifts from 'will this survive' to 'how do we make this faster.' Zcash has clearly caught a second wind this year, and this upgrade is the kind of unglamorous plumbing work that actually matters long after the hype cycle moves on.

Read more at Decrypt →

Visa Closes the Meme Coin Rewards Loophole

After banks lost their push for tighter stablecoin rules in the failed Clarity Act, JPMorgan scored a narrower win: Visa is moving to stop meme coin purchases from being coded as ordinary 'digital media' purchases, which had let them quietly rack up card rewards.

It's a small fix, but a telling one — traditional finance couldn't get the big regulatory win it wanted, so it's tightening things at the payment-rail level instead. Expect more of these quiet, unglamorous patches now that the big legislative swing has struck out.
