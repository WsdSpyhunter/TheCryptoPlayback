Bitcoin Rips to $85K, Bears Get Steamrolled

Bitcoin touched $85,000 for the first time since January as roughly $648 million in bearish crypto bets got liquidated in a short squeeze. Spot buyers reportedly kept the move going after the initial squeeze faded, and one measure of selling pressure sits near a record low.

Short squeezes are a great way to make a chart look euphoric without anyone actually having conviction — the real test is whether spot demand keeps showing up once the forced buyers are gone. Worth watching whether this was a one-week fireworks show or the start of something with actual staying power.

Clarity Act Dies, Banks and Dubai Throw a Party

The Senate's killing of the Clarity Act didn't just leave US crypto rules in limbo — critics argue it actively handed an advantage to traditional banks and offshore hubs like Dubai, who now get to operate in the regulatory gray zone that a clear US framework would have closed off.

This is the quiet cost of gridlock: while Congress fumbles the ball, capital and talent don't wait around, they just relocate to wherever the rules (or lack of them) are more convenient. Every year without clarity is another year US-based projects have to explain why they're not just incorporating somewhere friendlier.

Saylor's Strategy Gets 'A Little More Orange' Again

After a two-week pause, Strategy bought 950 bitcoin for $76 million, pushing total holdings to roughly 846,000 BTC — about 4% of the entire 21 million supply cap, worth around $72 billion. The company also repurchased $174 million of its STRC shares in the same stretch.

Saylor's buying cadence has become its own kind of market indicator at this point — when the pace picks back up, it tends to coincide with broader sentiment turning. Just remember that a corporate balance sheet strategy built for Strategy isn't a blueprint for anyone else's.

Tom Lee's Bitmine Keeps Stacking ETH, Declares the Bull Market Open

Bitmine added another $74 million in Ethereum, pushing its holdings to nearly 5.99 million ETH — about 4.9% of the entire supply. Chairman Tom Lee argued Ethereum's outperformance is a signal of a bigger move ahead, and that institutions remain underweight crypto broadly.

When a company owns nearly 5% of an asset's circulating supply, its chairman calling the market bullish is less an independent forecast and more a description of his own position. Doesn't make him wrong, but it's worth keeping in mind whose book gets fatter if the call plays out.

The ECB Builds Its Own Tokenization Rails

The European Central Bank deployed its Pontes platform to settle wholesale tokenized assets directly in central-bank money, skipping stablecoins entirely as the settlement layer.

This is the more boring but arguably more consequential kind of crypto news: central banks aren't waiting to be disrupted, they're just building their own version of the plumbing. Whether that ends up complementing or competing with the private stablecoin world is going to be one of the bigger structural questions of the next few years.

A French Family Held Hostage for $46,000 in Bitcoin

A crypto worker's children were held hostage in the latest French 'wrench attack,' with $46,000 taken. France remains the worst-hit country in the world for these physical crypto extortion incidents, with more than 70 logged in just the first eight months of 2026.

Every self-custody sermon in this space glosses over the fact that being your own bank also means being your own security detail, and that's an increasingly literal, physical-safety problem for people who are visibly holding crypto. This is the ugliest part of the industry and it deserves more attention than it gets.

Google and Apple Are Quietly Hiring for Crypto Rails

Big Tech is reportedly on the hunt for crypto talent, with Google and Apple both eyeing stablecoin and tokenization infrastructure as part of their future plans.

Big Tech doesn't chase trends it thinks are fads — it chases infrastructure it expects to matter for a decade. If Google and Apple are staffing up for stablecoins and tokenization now, that's a decent proxy for how mainstream this plumbing has already become, whether or not the average phone user ever notices.
