Bitcoin roared back above $85,000 on the back of a brutal short squeeze this week, while regulators, hackers, and Visa's compliance department all found their own reasons to make headlines.

Bitcoin Rips Past $85K as Shorts Get Wrecked

Bitcoin hit its first $85,000 print since January, with the move sparked by a short squeeze that liquidated roughly $648 million in bearish bets. Spot buyers reportedly kept the momentum going after the initial squeeze, and one measure of selling pressure sits near a record low.

Short squeezes are a great way to make a chart look heroic without anyone actually having conviction — it's forced buying, not necessarily belief. That said, when sellers this exhausted stop showing up, rallies have a funny way of sticking around longer than the skeptics expect.

Congress Fumbles Clarity Act, So the Regulators Grabbed the Wheel

The Clarity Act failed to advance in the Senate, and with it went crypto's best shot at a comprehensive legislative framework this cycle. Attention has now shifted to the SEC and CFTC, which are moving ahead with their own rulemaking rather than waiting on a gridlocked Congress.

This is the crypto version of 'we'll just do it ourselves' — and it's worth remembering that regulator-written rules can move faster than legislation, but they can also be undone faster too. Rulemaking by agency fiat is a foundation built on sand compared to an actual statute, even if it's the only foundation currently on offer.

Strategy Gets 'A Little More Orange' Again

Strategy bought another 950 bitcoin for $76 million, pushing its total holdings to roughly 846,000 BTC — about 4% of the entire 21 million supply cap and worth an estimated $72 billion. It's the company's return to buying after a pause.

At this point Strategy's bitcoin purchases are less news and more weather report — mostly clear, occasionally orange. The bigger story is what happens to corporate treasury strategy if this playbook stops being novel and starts being standard operating procedure for public companies.

Zcash's ETF Is Too Popular for Its Own Share Price

Grayscale's Zcash ETF is planning a 3-for-1 forward share split after pulling in more than $233 million in under a month, as the fund pushes toward $890 million in assets. The rally has also pushed ZEC mining competition to record highs.

Privacy coins spending years in the wilderness only to become 'Wall Street's hottest privacy trade' is one of those plot twists that would've gotten you laughed out of a crypto Twitter thread five years ago. Funny how a wrapper (an ETF) can make an old idea suddenly feel brand new to institutional money.

North Korean Hackers Are Still Handing Out Fake Job Offers

The North Korean cyber group WaterPlum reportedly infected 30,000 devices and stole $10.7 million in crypto by targeting developers with fake job offers at crypto, AI, and NFT companies.

The fake-recruiter scam has been running for years now and it still works, which says less about North Korean hackers being unstoppable geniuses and more about how many developers will click a suspicious link if the paycheck attached looks big enough. Basic security hygiene remains criminally underrated in this industry.

The ECB Wants Tokenization Without the Stablecoins

The European Central Bank launched Pontes, a platform to settle tokenized assets using central-bank money rather than stablecoins, with services set to expand gradually over time.

This is a pretty pointed statement from the ECB: they want the efficiency of tokenized settlement without ceding any ground to dollar-denominated stablecoins. It's a preview of how the tokenization boom might fracture along geopolitical lines rather than converging on one global rail.

Read more at Cointelegraph →

Visa Closes the Meme Coin Rewards Party

Visa is moving to stop meme coin purchases from being coded as ordinary 'digital media,' closing a loophole that let buyers rack up card rewards points on speculative token purchases. The move comes after banks lost their broader push for tighter stablecoin rules when the Clarity Act failed.

Somewhere out there is a person who genuinely earned airline miles buying meme coins, and Visa has decided that's a bridge too far. It's a small, almost comedic footnote to a much bigger regulatory fight, but it shows how payment networks are quietly drawing their own lines around crypto risk regardless of what Congress does.
