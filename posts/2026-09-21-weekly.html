Bitcoin Rips to $85K, Shorts Get Wrecked

Bitcoin touched $85,000 for the first time since January, with roughly $648 million in bearish bets liquidated as a short squeeze snowballed into sustained spot buying. Decrypt notes selling pressure is near a record low, suggesting the move has more behind it than just panicked shorts covering.

Short squeezes are great theater and genuinely good for sentiment, but they're also a reminder that a huge chunk of 'rallies' start as forced buying rather than conviction buying. Worth watching whether spot demand actually sticks once the leverage flush is done.

Strategy and Bitmine Keep Stacking Like It's Nothing

Strategy bought 950 more bitcoin for $76 million, pushing its total holdings to around 846,000 BTC — roughly 4% of the entire supply cap — while also repurchasing $174 million in STRC. Meanwhile Tom Lee's Bitmine added 27,562 ETH, bringing its total investments to $17.1 billion and its ether stake close to 5% of circulating supply.

Two different treasury strategies, same underlying bet: that being the biggest whale in the room is itself a moat. It's an aggressive concentration of supply in a handful of corporate balance sheets, which is either the ultimate vote of confidence or a structural risk nobody's pricing in yet, depending on your mood that day.

Clarity Act Dies in the Senate, Regulators Grab the Wheel

With the Clarity Act failing to advance, the industry's focus has shifted from Congress to the SEC and CFTC, which are now driving crypto policy through rulemaking rather than legislation. It's a meaningful shift in who actually writes the rules of the road.

Congress punting on crypto clarity isn't new, but watching two agencies fill the vacuum on their own timeline means the rules that eventually land will be shaped more by regulators' priorities than by any negotiated legislative compromise. That's a different game, with different winners.

CFTC Doesn't Wait Around for Congress

The CFTC submitted a prerule on crypto asset transactions and markets to the White House for review, signaling it plans to build a derivatives framework using its own authority now that the Clarity Act has stalled. This is regulation by agency initiative rather than statute.

Whatever you think of the outcome, there's something almost admirable about an agency just... deciding to do the job Congress won't. It also means the resulting framework may be narrower, faster, and considerably less durable than something passed into law — future administrations could reshape it just as unilaterally.

Grayscale's Zcash ETF Is So Hot It's Splitting Shares

Grayscale's Zcash ETF is doing a 3-for-1 forward share split after pulling in more than $233 million in under a month, making shares more affordable as Wall Street piles into what's being called crypto's hottest privacy trade.

Privacy coins spent years as the industry's awkward, semi-taboo corner. Watching institutional money flood into a Zcash wrapper via a regulated ETF wrapper is a genuinely funny plot twist — regulatory comfort catching up to a use case regulators used to eye warily.

ZetaChain Votes to Kill Its Own Blockchain

ZetaChain holders approved a plan to wind down the project's Cosmos-based layer 1 and migrate the ZETA token to Solana, with the team pivoting focus to Anuma, its private AI app. A final migration vote is still to come.

Shutting down your own chain to chase liquidity and attention on someone else's is an unusually honest admission from a project — building a standalone L1 just isn't the growth story it once was. Expect more 'independent chain' teams to quietly reach the same conclusion.

France's Wrench Attack Problem Isn't Slowing Down

A crypto worker's children were held hostage in the latest French 'wrench attack,' with $46,000 taken. France remains the world's worst-hit country for these physical extortion attacks, logging more than 70 incidents in just the first eight months of 2026.

Self-custody has an obvious upside, but stories like this are a blunt reminder that visible wealth — onchain or off — creates real-world targets. No amount of cold storage protects against someone showing up at your door, and that's a threat model too many people still don't take seriously.
