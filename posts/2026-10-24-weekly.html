Stablecoins Go Mainstream While Washington Sits Still

Top story

While a big bank quietly handed millions of everyday users a stablecoin wallet, Washington's crypto framework stayed stuck and Bitcoin spent the week consolidating under $82,000.

Here’s A Story You May Have Missed!

Your Water Heater Can Now Mine Bitcoin

Heatbit is selling an $899 water heater element called the Bitwater that swaps the usual resistance wire for 20 mining chips. The company says it uses the same electricity that would normally just heat your tank, turning a household appliance into a tiny miner. It's a strange but fitting example of how far bitcoin mining hardware has wandered from the data center and into the utility closet.

HSBC Just Made Stablecoins Boring (In a Good Way)

HSBC switched on its Hong Kong dollar stablecoin, RedCoin, inside PayMe on Friday, putting it in front of 3 million existing wallets for peer-to-peer and merchant payments, with plans to expand into tokenized investment subscriptions and institutional uses.

This is the kind of stablecoin news that doesn't trend on crypto Twitter but probably matters more than most of what does — a major bank making a token invisible enough that users won't even think of it as crypto. Quiet, unglamorous, and exactly how mass adoption tends to actually happen.

Read more at Decrypt →

America Sat Out Clarity Act, So Asia and the Gulf Are Taking the Seat

Apex Group's CEO argues that the U.S. Clarity Act's failure hands a competitive edge to Asia and the Middle East, as those regions move forward with clearer crypto rules while American legislation stalls.

Regulatory gridlock doesn't make crypto disappear, it just relocates where the infrastructure gets built. Every month Congress spends arguing is a month some other jurisdiction spends writing rulebooks that companies can actually build on.

Read more at CoinDesk →

Franklin Templeton Parks $150 Million in Fund Shares on the XRP Ledger

Franklin Templeton added $150 million in fund shares to the XRP Ledger, all deposited into a single wallet, signaling continued institutional interest in using the chain as settlement infrastructure for tokenized fund products.

One wallet, one asset manager, a very large number — it's a reminder that 'institutional adoption' these days often looks less like a press release and more like a quiet on-chain deposit you only notice if you're watching.

Read more at CoinDesk →

22 Companies Object to Tokenized Stock Listings Before They Even Launch

As of Friday, 22 companies — including Wells Fargo — had filed formal objections to the OKX-ICE joint venture's proposed tokenized stock platform, which is operating under the SEC's innovation exemption.

Tokenized equities have been pitched as the next obvious bridge between TradFi and crypto rails, but this is a good reminder that the companies whose stock gets tokenized get a vote too, and plenty of them aren't thrilled about it.

Read more at Cointelegraph →

New York Wants $1.37 Billion Back From a Ponzi Scheme That Used Prayer Groups

New York's attorney general is asking a court for a $1.37 billion judgment against the operators of NovaTech, a crypto Ponzi scheme that used prayer groups to lure victims and took in over $1.1 billion before its founders fled the country. Four top promoters have already settled for nearly $6 million.

The judgment is a number on paper until the money is actually recovered, and these founders are reportedly long gone. Still, it's a useful data point on just how much damage affinity-fraud-style crypto scams can do before anyone notices.

Read more at Bitcoin Magazine →

Netanyahu Floats a Blockchain Backup Plan for Bank Sanctions

Days before Israel's election, Netanyahu said he has asked for a blockchain plan in case Israeli banks are hit with sanctions, telling citizens to prepare for a rainy day after Trump.

Crypto as sanctions-evasion insurance is a use case that keeps resurfacing whenever geopolitics gets tense, and it says something about blockchain's actual utility that even heads of state are keeping it in the back pocket as a contingency, not a headline policy.

Read more at Decrypt →

Bitcoin Core Stops Pretending AI Can't Find Your Bugs

Bitcoin Core developers have rewritten their security policy so that fixes get backported and releases cut immediately for flaws flagged as 'easily discoverable,' abandoning the long embargo periods once given to bugs that an attacker can now surface with a single AI prompt.

This is a quietly significant acknowledgment that the old model of responsible disclosure assumed finding bugs was slow and hard. AI breaks that assumption, and it's good to see core infrastructure adjusting its defenses rather than just hoping the old playbook still holds.

Read more at Decrypt →

Bitcoin ETFs Bleed $636 Million as October Turns Red Again

Spot Bitcoin ETFs lost $635.6 million on Thursday, their biggest outflow of the month, pushing October's flows back into the red to about a $176 million loss for the month as Ethereum and Solana funds also bled.

A single-day outflow of this size is a useful reminder that ETF flows swing hard in both directions. It's a data point worth watching, not a verdict on anything.

Read more at Decrypt →

Three More Central Banks Join Singapore's Cross-Border Stablecoin Push

Malaysia and Thailand have joined Singapore's BLOOM initiative, a cross-border stablecoin and tokenized deposit settlement effort that also involves Ripple, Visa and Mastercard.

While the U.S. argues over a single piece of legislation, Southeast Asian central banks are quietly building the plumbing for cross-border stablecoin settlement together. It's the kind of multilateral infrastructure project that doesn't make headlines until it's already running.
