Regulators Fill the Clarity Act Void While Bitcoin ETFs Erase Their Bad Year

TOP STORY

With Congress punting on crypto clarity, agencies, exchanges, and miners are all quietly rewriting the rulebook themselves while ETF money floods back in.

Bitcoin ETFs Just Had Their Best Week Since October

Bitcoin ETFs pulled in $2.4 billion over the past week, their largest weekly haul since October, which is enough to push year-to-date flows back into positive territory after sitting roughly $5.8 billion underwater just two months ago.

It's a tidy reminder of how fast sentiment can flip in this market — the same funds that looked like a lost cause in July are now the story of the week. Whether it's a durable trend or a seven-day sugar rush is the kind of thing nobody actually knows until well after the fact.

Kalshi Loses Again, and Now It's Heading Toward the Supreme Court

The 6th US Circuit Court of Appeals ruled against Kalshi, siding with Ohio and Tennessee's argument that its sports event contracts aren't federally preempted swaps, deepening a circuit split and setting the stage for a potential Supreme Court showdown.

Prediction markets have spent the year acting like they've already won the regulatory argument by sheer momentum. Turns out state regulators and federal appeals courts have opinions too, and this fight is now officially bigger than any one exchange.

Read more at Cointelegraph →

'Crypto Mom' Is Leaving the Building

SEC Commissioner Hester Peirce, known for years as 'Crypto Mom' and the driving force behind the agency's Crypto Task Force, will depart her post on October 2 after roughly eight years at the SEC.

Peirce was the industry's most consistent internal ally at an agency that spent most of that time treating crypto like a problem to be litigated rather than regulated. Her exit lands right as agencies are racing to write crypto rules on their own after Congress failed to, which makes the timing feel less like coincidence and more like the end of an era.

Read more at Cointelegraph →

AI Agents Are Making Quantum-Safe Bitcoin Actually Affordable

An open competition run by StarkWare, Yukon Research, and Eigen Labs drove the estimated cost of a quantum-safe Bitcoin transaction from about $320 down to roughly $67, with AI models leading the leaderboard the whole way.

Quantum computing breaking Bitcoin's cryptography has always felt like a someday-problem, which is exactly the kind of problem that gets ignored until it isn't. Watching AI agents grind the cost of a fix down by nearly 80% in a competition is a genuinely useful signal that the someday-problem is getting cheaper to solve before it becomes a today-problem.

Read more at Decrypt →

Bitcoin Might Get Zcash-Style Privacy Without Touching the Base Protocol

New research suggests Bitcoin could gain Zcash-style 'shielded' privacy features without needing to change its underlying consensus rules, opening a path toward optional privacy that doesn't require a contentious fork.

Privacy on Bitcoin has always been the feature everyone agrees is important and nobody agrees on how to ship. A route that doesn't require rewriting the protocol's rules is the kind of pragmatic compromise that might actually survive contact with the Bitcoin community's famous allergy to change.

Read more at CoinDesk →

Circle Gets a Binance-Shaped Boost in the Stablecoin Wars

A new deal with Binance is giving Circle a leg up in its long-running stablecoin race against Tether, according to analysts tracking the arrangement.

Tether has had the stablecoin market pretty much to itself for years by sheer volume and network effect. Any deal that gets Circle deeper into the biggest exchange in the room is worth watching, even if 'boost' and 'dethrone' are still very different words.

Read more at CoinDesk →

Bitget Hacker Moves $83 Million in XRP That Ripple Literally Can't Freeze

Whoever hacked Bitget has moved $83 million worth of stolen XRP, and unlike some other tokens, this haul isn't something Ripple has the power to freeze or claw back.

It's a good reminder that 'decentralized' cuts both ways — the same properties that keep a network censorship-resistant also mean there's no customer-service line to call when things go wrong. Exchanges keep learning this lesson the expensive way.

Blockchain Association Shakes Up Leadership After Clarity Act Collapse

The Blockchain Association's CEO Summer Mersinger, a former CFTC Commissioner, is stepping down next month, with Kristin Smith returning as interim CEO shortly after the industry-backed Clarity Act failed to pass.

When the flagship legislation a lobbying group spent a year pushing for falls apart, leadership changes tend to follow. It's less a scandal than a gut check for the industry's DC strategy heading into whatever comes next.

Riot Platforms Pays Off Its $200M Debt, Keeps Building Data Centers

Riot Platforms has repaid its $200 million credit facility and released the collateral backing it, all while continuing to expand its data-center business alongside its core Bitcoin mining operations.

Miners diversifying into data centers has gone from a hedge to basically the standard playbook this cycle. Clearing a debt facility while still expanding is the kind of boring-but-healthy balance sheet move that doesn't make headlines for the right reasons, which is exactly why it's worth noting.
