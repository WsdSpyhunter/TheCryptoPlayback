Seizures, Lawsuits, and a Wallet Reseller Gone Rogue

Prices as of 6 AM CT on the issue date. See live indicators

Top story

Between billion-dollar sanctions seizures, a lifetime ban for Alex Mashinsky, and a hardware wallet scandal draining millions from users, crypto's 'Uptober' is proving a lot more eventful than its price chart suggests.

Here’s A Story You May Have Missed!

Former FTX COO Says Netflix's FTX Drama Made Her Physically Ill

Former FTX COO Constance Wang says the trailer for Netflix's 'The Altruists' made her 'physically throw up,' arguing it misrepresents FTX's collapse by turning it into a love story. It's a strange little footnote in the FTX saga: the people who lived through one of crypto's biggest implosions now have to watch it get reimagined as streaming entertainment, and apparently the Hollywood version doesn't match their memory of events at all.
Read more at The Block →

Treasury Says It's Coming for $1 Billion in Iran-Linked Crypto

Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent announced plans to seize roughly $1 billion in crypto tied to Iran sanctions this week, part of a broader wave of digital asset seizures the Treasury has been rolling out.

Sanctions enforcement moving onto the blockchain isn't new, but a billion-dollar figure said out loud by the Treasury Secretary is a different kind of headline than the usual quiet wallet freeze. It's a reminder that 'crypto is untraceable' was always more marketing slogan than fact.

Read more at Cointelegraph →

Mashinsky Gets the Industry Ban He's Earned

New York has permanently barred former Celsius CEO Alex Mashinsky from the securities and crypto industries as part of a $35 million settlement resolving the state's 2023 civil fraud lawsuit against him.

Celsius promised yield that didn't exist and a CEO who apparently didn't either, once the lights came on. A permanent ban feels almost anticlimactic at this point, but it's still a notable bookend to one of the uglier chapters of the last cycle.

Read more at Cointelegraph →

Ledger's Reseller Problem Just Became an $86 Million Problem

Ledger is investigating reports that users in Southeast Asia lost fund after buying hardware wallets from a reseller called CryptoBilis, with an onchain investigator tracking more than $86 million in suspected thefts. Ledger has asked the reseller to pause sales and is warning recent buyers not to set up their devices.

Hardware wallets are supposed to be the boring, bulletproof part of self-custody. A compromised supply chain undercuts the entire premise, and it's a brutal lesson in checking exactly where your 'cold storage' device actually came from.

Read more at Cointelegraph →

THORChain Exec Says Tether Froze Then Quietly Unfroze USDT Vaults

THORChain's technical co-founder Chad Barraford says Tether temporarily froze USDT vaults on the network before unfreezing them without explanation.

Tether freezing funds isn't unusual on its own—it does that for sanctions and law enforcement requests all the time. What's notable here is the lack of explanation, which is exactly the kind of opacity that keeps 'is my stablecoin actually censorship-resistant' debates alive.

Read more at Cointelegraph →

Blockchain.com Wants to Run Its Own Prediction Markets, CFTC Permitting

Blockchain.com has filed for designated contract market and futures commission merchant licenses, which would let it operate a regulated event-contract venue in the US instead of leaning on overseas partners—reportedly ahead of a planned $500 million IPO.

Prediction markets have gone from crypto curiosity to genuine regulatory battleground this year, and everyone wants to be the compliant option before Washington picks winners. Getting there first, and legally, might matter more than getting there loudest.

Read more at Decrypt →

Starknet Pops 20% on Plan to Ditch Ethereum L2 Status

Starknet jumped 20% after saying it's 'actively considering' becoming its own standalone blockchain rather than an Ethereum layer-2, a move it claims would make it the first fully quantum-resistant network by 2027.

Going independent is a big strategic swing for an L2, and the quantum-resistance angle is clearly aimed at getting ahead of a threat most chains are still pretending is decades away. Whether traders are pricing in the roadmap or just the headline is the real question.

Read more at Decrypt →

Bitcoin Bleeds in Uptober, and Traders Aren't Convinced It's Done

Bitcoin has slid in what's traditionally a seasonally strong month, with analysts pointing to support levels ranging from $83,000 down to $72,000 that it may still need to test.

'Uptober' was always more meme than guarantee, and this year's price action is a solid reminder that seasonal patterns are backward-looking observations, not forward-looking promises.

Read more at Decrypt →

DWF Labs Affiliates Sue BitGo for $141 Million Over Early Token Dumps

DWF Labs-linked firms are suing BitGo in London's High Court, alleging it sold discounted Falcon Finance and ESPORTS tokens roughly two months before their lock-up expired, tanking the value of their remaining holdings.

Token lock-ups are supposed to be the guardrail that prevents exactly this kind of scenario. If the allegations hold up, it's a pointed reminder that custodial and market-making arrangements deserve as much scrutiny as the tokens themselves.
