Uptober Opens Green While an Old Foe Gets a New Job

Top story

Bitcoin is holding its ground against a surging dollar and a parade of corporate treasury theatrics, while Washington quietly rearranges the deck chairs on crypto oversight.

Saylor's Strategy Keeps Stacking, Now Owns 4% of All Bitcoin Ever

Strategy added another 334 BTC for $28.7 million, pushing its total holdings past 848,000 BTC — roughly 4% of the entire 21 million supply cap, worth about $73 billion. The buys keep coming regardless of market mood, which at this point feels less like a strategy and more like a personality.

Concentration like this cuts both ways: it's a remarkable show of conviction, but it also means an awful lot of bitcoin's story is now tied to one company's balance sheet decisions.

Metaplanet Sells 10,000 BTC, Buys Back 11,000, Calls It a Demo

Metaplanet offloaded 10,000 BTC in Q3 and then repurchased 11,000, netting a gain of 1,000 BTC and bringing total holdings to 44,000 BTC. CEO Simon Gerovich framed the whole exercise as answering rating agencies' doubts about whether a bitcoin treasury company would actually sell when obligations come due: 'We answered by doing it.'

It's a clever bit of financial theater — proving liquidity by voluntarily creating and resolving your own stress test. Whether regulators and rating agencies find it reassuring or a little too cute is the real question here.

Read more at Decrypt →

OKX and NYSE's Parent Want to Trade Stocks Around the Clock, On-Chain

A joint venture between OKX and ICE (NYSE's owner) has filed with the SEC under its new Innovation Exemption to launch 24/7 tokenized trading for more than 60 US-listed stocks — including Nvidia and SpaceX — paired with stablecoins.

This is the kind of filing that quietly matters more than a price pump: traditional market infrastructure and crypto rails formally shaking hands. If it goes live as described, 'market hours' starts to become a legacy concept.

Read more at Decrypt →

Treasury Crackdown Finds Crypto's Fingerprints on an $8M Hamas Fundraising Network

A Treasury Department crackdown has surfaced crypto's role in a roughly $2 million Hamas fundraising network, adding another entry to the growing file of illicit-finance cases that keep crypto in regulators' crosshairs.

Stories like this are exactly why 'crypto is just for criminals' narratives persist, even as the vast majority of activity on-chain is mundane. The tech's transparency is also what makes these networks traceable in the first place — a point that often gets lost in the headline.

Read more at CoinDesk →

Trump Puts the Man Who Sued Ripple in Charge of AI Policy

President Trump has named former SEC Chair Jay Clayton — who as SEC chief launched the agency's lawsuit against Ripple — to lead a new 'Super Intelligence Force' coordinating federal AI policy. Clayton currently serves as Director of National Intelligence.

The irony writes itself: the regulator crypto loves to hate is now steering a different frontier industry's oversight. Whether that's a demotion from crypto's perspective or just Washington's game of musical chairs is anyone's guess.

Read more at Decrypt →

Community Banks Sue to Shut Crypto's 'Side Door' Into the Banking System

The Independent Community Bankers of America has filed suit arguing that the OCC's national trust charters give crypto firms a 'side door into the banking system' without the safeguards that bind traditional banks.

This is the turf war that was always coming — legacy banking lobbies versus crypto firms angling for bank-like privileges without bank-like rules. However it shakes out, it'll shape how easily crypto companies can plug into the traditional financial system going forward.

Read more at Decrypt →

Ethereum Stakers Are Stuck in a Two-Week Line to Get Out

Nearly 1.5 million ETH is currently queued in Ethereum's staking exit line, with wait times stretching around 25 days, according to CoinDesk.

It's a reminder that staking liquidity isn't instant, no matter how the yield gets marketed. A system designed for security and stability can also mean your exit plan takes weeks, not minutes.

Read more at CoinDesk →

Uptober Opens With $134 Million Flowing Into Bitcoin ETFs

Spot Bitcoin ETFs pulled in $134.4 million over the first two trading days of October, bouncing back from a Sept. 30 outflow after a weak jobs report cooled expectations of a Fed rate hike.

Seasonal nicknames like 'Uptober' are fun shorthand, but two days of inflows is a pretty thin data set to build a narrative on. Worth watching whether the flows hold up once the jobs-report sugar high wears off.

Hacker Returns $3.8 Million After Getting a 48-Hour Ultimatum

Near Intents said the roughly $3.8 million drained in a Thursday exploit was returned in full after the team told the attacker 'We have identified you, sir' and gave a 48-hour deadline to send it back. It's a strangely polite resolution to what's usually a messy, protracted saga of negotiations and bounty offers. Sometimes on-chain forensics plus a firm deadline is all it takes.
