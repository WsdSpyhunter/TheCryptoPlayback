Here’s A Story You May Have Missed!

A Blockchain That Got Hacked, Then Voted to Just Restart

Qubic restarted its blockchain and opened recovery claims after a $1.4 million hack, with validators approving a recovery plan and setting a claims window open until May 1, 2027. It's a strange middle ground between decentralization theater and a corporate insurance process, with network participants essentially voting to roll back the clock and give victims years to file paperwork. Not quite the 'code is law' ethos, but also not nothing.
Read more at Cointelegraph →

OCC Finally Drops Its GENIUS Act Stablecoin Rulebook

The OCC finalized its GENIUS Act stablecoin rule ahead of the January deadline, laying out reserve, redemption and risk standards for issuers. The agency still hasn't said when it will start accepting applications, so the starting gun for actual issuance remains unfired.

Having the rulebook is progress, but 'we'll tell you when we're ready' is not exactly the regulatory clarity the industry has been begging for since 2021.

Read more at Decrypt →

JPMorgan Finally Puts a Number on Bitcoin, Then Hedges It

J.P. Morgan Asset Management's 2027 Long-Term Capital Market Assumptions project a 10% annual return for Bitcoin over 10 to 15 years, though the firm isn't adding it to its main portfolio models.

It's a milestone that a bank this size puts a formal figure on bitcoin at all, but the asterisk is doing a lot of work here. Having a number doesn't mean having a recommendation.

Read more at Decrypt →

Dogecoin Tries to Grow Up With Its Own Lab

Dogecoin Labs, backed by the Dogecoin Foundation, unites MyDoge, DogeOS and Such App to push DOGE into payments and DeFi, an attempt to give the meme coin everyday uses beyond price speculation.

DOGE has survived on culture and community for a decade; building real payment rails underneath it is a different kind of bet entirely, and a much harder one to pull off than minting another meme.

Read more at The Block →

Zcash's November Upgrade Has a Date and the Market Noticed

Zcash developers confirmed a November date for an upgrade that triples payment speed, part of the broader NU7 overhaul that's helped fuel the asset's recent gains.

Privacy coins spend most cycles as an afterthought, so watching ZEC actually move on real technical news rather than pure narrative is a nice change of pace.

Read more at CoinDesk →

North Korea-Linked Drift Hacker Keeps Feeding Tornado Cash

Wallets linked to the $295 million April Drift hack sent another $109 million in Ether to Tornado Cash over two days, after already moving $46 million through the mixer on Wednesday. The wallets still held $138 million in Ether afterward.

Watching a nine-figure hack slowly launder itself in public, in real time, in full view of on-chain analysts, is a strange kind of transparency. It doesn't stop the money moving, but at least everyone can watch it happen.

Read more at Cointelegraph →

A SpaceX-Tesla Merger Would Also Merge Two Huge Bitcoin Stacks

SpaceX and Tesla are exploring a merger that would unite more than 30,000 bitcoin between the two companies' balance sheets.

Beyond the corporate logic of folding Musk's empire into one entity, this is a reminder that some of the largest bitcoin holdings in the world now sit inside publicly traded companies with very public founders, which is its own kind of concentration risk worth watching.

Read more at CoinDesk →

Utah Calls Kalshi a 'Back-Alley Gambling Den' in New Lawsuit

Utah's Attorney General sued prediction market company Kalshi, naming Susquehanna firms and seeking to recover alleged gambling profits while likening the platform to a 'public nuisance' causing ongoing harm to state residents.

Prediction markets keep winning federal-level legitimacy while individual states dig in their heels, and that mismatch isn't resolving itself quietly.

Read more at Cointelegraph →

Microsoft, Alphabet and Goldman Say Not So Fast on Tokenized Stocks

Sixteen companies have now objected to having their shares traded on the planned OKX-ICE tokenized stock venue, up from nine just four days earlier, according to an updated public notice.

Tokenization keeps getting pitched as inevitable, but when the actual issuers start lining up to say 'not with our shares, thanks,' that inevitability looks a lot less settled than the pitch decks suggest.
