Jobs Data Wins, Regulators Sue, Layer-2s Die

TOP STORY

A soft jobs report sent Bitcoin sprinting toward its yearly highs even as regulators, banks, and an entire layer-2 network reminded everyone that crypto's plumbing is still very much under construction.

Chainalysis Says AI Helped It Chase Down North Korea's $387M Bitget Haul

Chainalysis used in-house AI to trace the stolen funds from the Sept. 24 Bitget breach across four blockchains, racing the attackers in real time. The firm says this pushes North Korea's 2026 crypto haul past $1 billion.

It's a genuinely useful application of AI — chasing money through mixers and bridges faster than humans can manually untangle them — but the bigger story is the number itself. A billion dollars and counting means the deterrence math still isn't working, no matter how good the forensics get.

Trump Throws Another Meme Coin Dinner, Timing Be Damned

The TRUMP meme coin project is inviting its top 185 holders to a Nov. 22 gala with the president — two weeks after ethics disputes over his crypto interests helped stall the Clarity Act in the Senate.

There's something almost defiant about scheduling this particular dinner right after the conflict-of-interest arguments torpedoed a major piece of crypto legislation. Whatever you think of the policy stakes, the optics write themselves.

Bitcoin Pops Past $87K on a Lousy Jobs Report

Bitcoin briefly touched $87,000 after September payrolls came in at just 29,000 with unemployment ticking up to 4.2% — weak enough data to send bond yields lower and odds of an October rate hike toward zero.

It's the same old dance: bad news for the broader economy reads as good news for risk assets hunting for looser monetary policy. Watching BTC's price action track the labor market this closely is a reminder that, macro narrative aside, it still trades like a very liquid, very online bet on central bank behavior.

Once a $2.3 Billion Network, Blast Calls It Quits

Ethereum layer-2 Blast is shutting down after operating costs outpaced the revenue it generates, asking users to withdraw to mainnet before Oct. 26. The network's assets have reportedly plunged 98% from their peak.

Blast launched with huge incentive programs and bigger promises; now it's a cautionary tale about what happens when the subsidies run out and the usage never quite shows up to replace them. L2 proliferation was supposed to be about scaling Ethereum — turns out it's also about learning which chains actually had product-market fit.

NEAR Intents Gets Its $3.8M Back After Giving the Hacker an Ultimatum

The exploiter who drained NEAR Intents returned the entire stolen sum after being identified — a rare case where the ultimatum actually worked and the funds came home in full.

Chalk this up as one of the more satisfying outcomes in a year full of nine-figure hacks. It doesn't fix the underlying vulnerability, but a full recovery with a one-hour patch turnaround is about as good a worst-case scenario as this industry gets.

Read more at Cointelegraph →

Community Banks Sue Federal Regulator Over Crypto Trust Charters

A community banking group is suing the OCC, claiming the regulator overstepped its mandate by granting trust bank charters to crypto firms.

This is turf war dressed up as legal theory, but the underlying question — who gets to call itself a bank, and under what rules — is exactly the kind of fight that will shape how deeply crypto firms can integrate with the traditional financial system. Expect more of these lawsuits before fewer.

Read more at Cointelegraph →

BitGo CEO Says Clarity Act's Failure Left Markets Exposed to a Lehman-Style Risk

BitGo's Mike Belshe warned that the Clarity Act's collapse left systemic risk on the table, specifically pointing to firms that combine exchange, brokerage, and custody functions under one roof.

It's a pointed warning from someone whose business model depends on separating those functions. Whether or not you buy the Lehman comparison, the structural concern — too much concentrated in too few hands with too little oversight — is one regulators keep circling back to without actually resolving.

Read more at The Block →

Spot Bitcoin ETFs Pull in $2.7 Billion in September

US spot bitcoin ETFs logged $2.65 billion in net inflows for September, their second-largest monthly haul since October 2025, signaling institutional demand is holding up.

Steady ETF inflows have become the quiet backbone of this cycle's bid — less flashy than a meme coin pump, but arguably more consequential for where the asset's ownership base ends up over the long run.
