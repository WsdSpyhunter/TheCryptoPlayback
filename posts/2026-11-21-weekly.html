Here’s A Story You May Have Missed!

A GTA 6 Leaker Is Holding a PC Port Hostage for a Crypto Pump

An anonymous leaker going by Cyberleek released a 209 GB Xbox build of GTA 6 but is withholding the executable that could enable a PC port until a token called CYBERLEEK hits $15 million. It's an odd mashup of gaming piracy and token speculation, essentially turning a leak into a market cap target. Whether anyone actually gets a PC port out of this, or just a lesson in how far people will go to pump a coin, remains to be seen.
Read more at Decrypt →

Bitcoin Tanks Below $57,000, First Time in Two Years

Bitcoin fell below $57,000 for the first time in two years, part of a broader selloff that dragged other assets down with it, according to CoinDesk.

Two-year lows tend to bring out two kinds of people: the ones calling it a generational entry point and the ones calling it the start of something worse. Neither camp has special knowledge right now, and that's worth remembering before trusting either one.

Read more at CoinDesk →

Nvidia Crashes 48% as the AI Trade Gets a Reality Check

Tech stocks buckled after Anthropic's historic IPO and a leadership crisis at Nvidia, whose shares crashed 48%, while Bitcoin slipped below $60,000 alongside the rout. Experts quoted by Decrypt disagree on whether this is a correction or a dot-com-style bust.

Crypto spent years insisting it wasn't just a leveraged bet on risk appetite, and then AI showed up and proved that theory wrong in real time. When Nvidia sneezes, apparently Bitcoin still catches a cold.

Read more at Decrypt →

Chainflip's FLIP Tanks 30% After Suspected Vault Drain

Cross-chain protocol Chainflip's FLIP token tanked 30% after a suspected $4.86 million vault drain, per CoinDesk.

Cross-chain bridges and vaults remain one of the industry's favorite places to lose money that isn't theirs. Every exploit like this is a reminder that 'cross-chain' is often just a fancier word for 'more places for a key to go wrong.'

Read more at CoinDesk →

Wrapped Monero Collapses 99.8% in Suspected $13.5M Exploit

Cointelegraph reports Bitquery traced $13.5 million in suspicious sales of a wrapped Monero token, whose price crashed 99.8%, as its operator blamed a security breach and promised to reimburse affected traders.

Wrapping privacy coins onto transparent chains always felt like a neat workaround until something exactly like this happened. A promise to reimburse is nice, but it's still a promise, not a transaction.

Read more at Cointelegraph →

SEC Says Fake Exchanges Bilked Investors Out of $37 Million

Decrypt reports the SEC alleges eight Colorado-registered entities, likely run from overseas, posed as legitimate advisers and crypto platforms—even faking SEC certificates and filings—to defraud U.S. investors of more than $37 million.

Faking an SEC certificate is a bold move, and apparently a lucrative one until it isn't. It's a good reminder that the paperwork looking official has never been proof of anything.

Read more at Decrypt →

Senate Passes 'Death Bets Act' to Ban War and Death Wagers

The U.S. Senate passed a bill to ban prediction market bets on war and death, according to CoinDesk, aiming to stop contracts tied to assassination, terrorism and military events amid concerns over potential insider trading.

Prediction markets have had a rough stretch of credibility questions, and legislating away the grimmest categories of contracts is an easy call politically. Whether it actually curbs insider trading elsewhere in the space is a separate question entirely.

Read more at CoinDesk →

Hong Kong Moves to License Crypto Custodians and Advisers

Hong Kong's securities regulator is pushing to bring crypto custodians and advisers under licensing rules, with the bill set for its first reading in the Legislative Council on Dec. 2, Cointelegraph reports.

Hong Kong keeps building out a comprehensive licensing stack while other jurisdictions argue about definitions. It's a slow, unglamorous way to become the place institutions actually trust with custody.

Read more at Cointelegraph →

AI Finds a Bug That Could Stall Bitcoin Nodes With One Repeated ID

CoinDesk reports Bitcoin nodes could be stalled by repeating one ID 50,000 times, a bug that was found by AI.

Having an AI catch a bug like this before an attacker does is the kind of win that doesn't make headlines the way a hack does, but it's exactly the unglamorous work that keeps the base layer boring in the best way possible.
